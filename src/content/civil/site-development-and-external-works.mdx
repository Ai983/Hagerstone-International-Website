## What it covers

| Element | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Survey and levels | Everything is set out from them |
| Earthworks | Cut and fill balance drives cost |
| Surface water drainage | Discharge point dictates falls site-wide |
| Foul drainage | Route and connection to the public system |
| Roads and hardstanding | Access, loading, turning, fire appliances |
| Incoming services | Power, water, gas, telecoms — long lead times |
| Boundary and security | Walls, fencing, gates, gatehouse |
| Landscaping | Drainage, shading, environmental requirements |

## Start with the discharge point

Surface water drainage is the constraint that quietly sets the whole site.

Water moves by gravity to a discharge point — an existing drain, a watercourse,
or a soakaway where ground conditions permit. The **level of that discharge
point** determines the falls achievable across the site, which constrains road
levels, which constrains the finished floor level of the building.

Designing levels first and finding the discharge point afterwards is how sites
end up with pumped drainage that nobody budgeted for. Plumbing and drainage
provisions sit in **NBC 2016, Part 9**.

## Finished floor level is nearly irreversible

It follows from existing ground levels, the access road, drainage falls, flood
considerations where applicable, and how vehicles reach dock doors.

Once the slab is cast, none of those can be revisited. Getting it wrong produces
docks at the wrong height for the vehicles that will use them, or a site where
water runs toward the building rather than away from it.

## Earthworks: the cut and fill balance

The cheapest earthworks arrangement is usually the one where material cut from
high areas fills low ones, so little is imported or removed.

That balance depends on the level chosen, which is another reason level-setting
is an early cost decision rather than a survey formality. Unsuitable material
also has to be identified — a geotechnical investigation is what distinguishes
"we can reuse this" from "this has to be removed and replaced".

## Roads, hardstanding and fire access

Different areas take different duty:

- General circulation roads
- Loading and dock areas with braking and turning forces
- Parking, which is comparatively light
- **Fire tender access** — width, turning, standing capacity and proximity

Fire access is a design constraint rather than an afterthought, determined under
**NBC 2016, Part 4** by the fire consultant and the authority having
jurisdiction. Discovering it after the site layout is fixed means rearranging
roads that are already built.

Surfacing follows the duty — concrete to **IS 456:2000**, paving blocks to
**IS 15658**, or bituminous construction — with docks and turning areas usually
warranting heavier construction than the general yard.

## Incoming services govern the operating date

Electrical supply capacity and connection, water supply, effluent discharge
consent and gas connections all involve external parties.

These are outside your control, have long and uncertain timelines, and regularly
determine when a facility can actually operate. They belong on the programme
from the first week with named owners — not in the services package to be
resolved later.

## Standards referenced

Concrete practice in **IS 456:2000**; drainage provisions in
**NBC 2016, Part 9**; fire fighting access provisions in **NBC 2016, Part 4**;
precast concrete paving blocks in **IS 15658**. Levels, drainage design, road
construction, fire access requirements and utility connections for a specific
site must be established by the project's civil engineer and consultants, and
confirmed with the relevant authorities and utility providers.
