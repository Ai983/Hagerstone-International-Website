## What we deliver

**Point-fixed structural glazing** using spider routels, with glass fins and tie
rods as the supporting structure where the design calls for maximum
transparency.

Spider glazing has the least margin for error of any glazing system on a
project. Each panel hangs from four bolted points, holes are drilled before
toughening and cannot be changed, and there is no frame to conceal a
dimensional error. Everything has to be correct at fabrication.

## Scope of works

| Stage | What we deliver |

## What drives the cost

Glass thickness, set by span and wind pressure · fitting type — countersunk
routels cost more than standard bolts and give a flush face · whether the
back-up is glass fin, tension rod or steel · panel size · height and access ·
heat soaking.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Non-heat-soaked toughened glass.** Nickel sulphide inclusions cause
  spontaneous breakage years later, over an occupied space.
- **Setting out from the building rather than the glass.** Holes are fixed at
  fabrication; the structure must be surveyed and built to match.
- **Under-designed back-up structure.** Spiders transfer the entire wind load
  into whatever is behind them.
- **Rigid fittings.** Fittings must articulate, or bending moment is forced into
  a drilled hole — the weakest point in the panel.
- **Specifying it across a full elevation.** High cost, weak thermal
  performance, difficult sealing.
