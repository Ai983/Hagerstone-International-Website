## What we deliver

**Aluminium, stainless steel, mild steel and glass railing systems** —
balustrades, balcony railings, terrace and staircase railings, engineered to the
imposed loading for the occupancy rather than assembled to a standard detail.

A railing is a protective barrier before it is a design element. It is one of
the few facade components where a specification error puts a person at direct
risk, which is why the load case and the base fixing get designed rather than
assumed.

## What drives the cost

Running length · material — SS, aluminium, MS or frameless glass · stainless
grade (304 versus 316 for coastal or external exposure) · glass build-up and
thickness · whether posts are surface-mounted or core-drilled · handrail profile
and joint detailing · site access.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Single toughened glass in a barrier.** If it fails, the barrier is gone.
  Laminated construction keeps the panel in place.
- **Base fixings not designed for the load.** The anchor into the slab is the
  weakest point in most failed railings, and it is invisible once finished.
- **304 stainless in coastal or heavily polluted exposure.** It pits and
  tea-stains; 316 is the grade for those environments.
- **Post spacing set by appearance.** Spacing follows from the design load and
  the glass or infill span, not from what looks balanced on an elevation.
- **MS railing with an inadequate coating system.** Economical on day one,
  rusting within a couple of monsoons.
