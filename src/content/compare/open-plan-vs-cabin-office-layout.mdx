## Reframing the question

Leadership teams usually debate this as a culture question — open plan for
collaboration, cabins for hierarchy and focus.

The planning reality is more concrete. Every enclosed room **takes area, blocks
daylight, adds services and changes the compliance picture.** Every open desk
**saves area and light but has no acoustic privacy.** The useful discussion is
about how many enclosed rooms the floor genuinely needs, of what size, and
where they sit.

## Side by side

| Factor | Open plan | Cabin-led |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Area per person | Lower | Higher |
| Daylight reach into the floor | Good | Blocked, especially at the facade |
| Acoustic privacy | Poor without support spaces | Good |
| Services cost | Lower | Higher — air, lighting, detection per room |
| Reconfiguration | Easy | Partitions must be moved or demolished |
| Sense of status | Flat | Explicit |
| Suits | Collaborative, changing teams | Confidential work, fixed structure |
| Fire and escape implications | Simpler | Must be re-checked for subdivision |
| Growth headroom | Absorbs additional desks | Fixed room count |

## Where the cabin cost really lands

A cabin looks like a partition and a door. On a fit-out budget it is:

- The partition, door and ironmongery
- Its own supply air and a return path
- A lighting circuit, switching and controls
- Ceiling grid adaptation, diffuser relocation, sprinkler and detector changes
- The circulation needed to reach it
- The daylight it removes from everyone behind it

That is why the cost difference between a layout with six cabins and one with
sixteen is much larger than six versus sixteen partitions.

## Daylight is the decision most often made backwards

The instinct is to give the best light to the most senior people, which puts
cabins along the facade.

The consequence is that daylight stops at that line, and the open floor behind
runs on artificial light all day. This affects how the office feels more than
almost any other planning decision, and it works against the lighting and
control provisions of **ECBC 2017**, where daylight-linked control depends on
daylight actually reaching the space.

Two workable alternatives: put enclosed rooms toward the core, or glaze them so
light passes through. Interior illumination practice is covered in **IS 3646**,
and the design should be set with the lighting consultant.

## Open plan fails without support spaces

The complaint about open plan is almost always a complaint about the absence of
somewhere else to go.

A floor that provides enclosed meeting rooms in several sizes, a few focus
rooms, phone booths for calls, and absorbent surfaces performs entirely
differently from an open floor with nothing but desks. **ISO 3382-3** addresses
measurement of acoustic conditions in open-plan offices, and the practical
levers are the ceiling, the floor finish, screens and the distance between
speaking positions.

Budget the support spaces as part of choosing open plan, not as an
afterthought when complaints start.

## Mistakes that cost real money

- **Fixing the layout before compliance is checked.** Subdivision changes escape
  and detection arrangements; it is cheap to solve on a drawing and expensive
  after ceilings close.
- **Cabins along the entire facade.** The whole floor behind them loses daylight.
- **Open plan with no quiet rooms.** Guarantees the complaint the layout was
  supposed to avoid.
- **Counting seats in MEP and circulation zones.** Produces a headline seat
  number that collapses when the services layout is coordinated.
- **Planning for today's headcount only.** A layout with no growth allowance
  forces a reconfiguration within the same lease.
- **Deciding room sizes without testing them on the actual plan.** Standard
  allowances rarely survive contact with a real floor plate and core position.

## Standards referenced

Fire and life safety, including occupancy, exits and travel distance, in
**NBC 2016, Part 4**; lighting and control provisions in **ECBC 2017**; interior
illumination practice in **IS 3646**; open-plan acoustic measurement in
**ISO 3382-3**. Escape arrangements, occupancy assessment and detection layout
for a specific floor must be determined by the project's fire consultant and the
authority having jurisdiction.
