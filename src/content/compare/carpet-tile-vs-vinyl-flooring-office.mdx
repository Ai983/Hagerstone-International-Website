    answer: "It depends on what wears them out. Vinyl resists abrasion and scuffing better and is the usual choice for main circulation, entrances and areas with trolleys or chairs on hard castors. Carpet tile in a heavily trafficked corridor flattens and soils in the traffic lane.   - question: "Which is easier to maintain?"
    answer: "Vinyl for cleaning — it wipes, it handles spills and it suits pantries, cafeterias and anywhere food and liquid are present.     answer: "Considerably. Carpet tile is more tolerant of minor irregularity. Underestimating vinyl's screed preparation is one of the most common cost surprises in a fit-out."

## This is usually not an either-or

The best-performing office floors use both, deliberately zoned: **carpet where
people sit and think, hard flooring where people walk, carry and spill.**

Framing it as a single choice for the whole plate is what produces either a
noisy office or a floor that stains in the first month.

## Side by side

| Factor | Carpet tile | Luxury vinyl tile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Footfall noise | Absorbs | Reflects |
| Speech reflection | Absorbs | Reflects |
| Spill and stain handling | Needs prompt attention | Wipes clean |
| Abrasion resistance | Lower — traffic lanes flatten | Higher |
| Spot repair | Swap a single tile | Cut, patch, reweld |
| Subfloor tolerance | Forgiving | Demanding — must be flat |
| Works over a raised floor | Well — lifts with panels | Needs careful detailing |
| Suits pantry and cafe | No | Yes |
| Suits open-plan desking | Yes | Acoustically poor |
| Cleaning regime | Vacuum, periodic deep clean | Wipe and mop |

## Acoustics is the argument people underweight

Open-plan floors fail on noise more than on any other physical attribute, and
the complaint arrives months after handover, when it is expensive to fix.

The absorbent surfaces available on a typical floor are the ceiling, any wall
treatment, and the floor. Where the ceiling is hard and partitions are glazed —
a very common modern specification — the floor becomes the largest absorbent
area in the room.

Choosing hard flooring across an open-plan floor in that situation is a decision
to build a reverberant office. Sound absorption is measured under **ISO 354**
and reported as absorption data for the product, so this is assessable at
specification stage rather than discoverable after occupation.

## Subfloor preparation: the hidden cost line

Vinyl is thin. Anything under it shows through — trowel ridges, joints, patches,
differences in level.

That means screeding, levelling compound and proper preparation, and the cost is
real. A quotation comparing carpet tile and vinyl on material rate alone,
without the subfloor preparation vinyl needs, is not a comparison. On a
refurbishment with an uneven existing slab, preparation can be the largest part
of the difference.

## Durability, honestly

Vinyl wears better. Carpet flattens and soils along traffic lanes, which is
visible long before the tile is worn out.

But carpet tile's **replaceability** is a genuine counterweight. The worn lane
is lifted and replaced with new tiles, or rotated with tiles from a low-traffic
area, and the floor looks new again without closing it. Keeping attic stock from
the original batch is what makes this work — and the batch matters, because dye
lots do not match perfectly years later.

## Mistakes that cost real money

- **One finish across the whole plate.** Zone it: carpet at desks, hard flooring
  in circulation, pantry and cafe.
- **Comparing material rates without subfloor preparation.** Vinyl's real cost
  includes getting the substrate flat.
- **Hard floor plus hard ceiling plus glass partitions.** Three reflective
  surfaces produce an office nobody wants to work in.
- **No attic stock.** Without it, the spot-replacement advantage of carpet tile
  is lost, and a colour match years later is unlikely.
- **Bonding vinyl over access panels.** It makes the raised floor unusable for
  the purpose it was installed for.
- **Ignoring entrance matting.** Grit tracked in from outside is what actually
  wears both finishes; a properly sized entrance matting zone protects the whole
  floor.

## Standards referenced

Textile floor covering classification to **EN 1307**; resilient floor covering
classification by use to **EN ISO 10874**; sound absorption measurement to
**ISO 354**; surface finish and fire provisions in **NBC 2016, Part 4**.
Applicable fire requirements for floor coverings in a specific building,
particularly on escape routes, must be confirmed by the project's fire
consultant and the authority having jurisdiction.
