## The brief

A financial-services occupier taking a single long floor plate in Netaji Subhash Place,
New Delhi, for its head office. One floor had to hold the executive suite, the meeting
and boardroom load a lending business runs, a client-facing lounge, and as many
workstations as the plate would take without the floor becoming unpleasant to work on.

The plate is a deep rectangle with the core at one end. That geometry decides the plan
before anyone draws furniture: the only sensible move is two long bands of desks either
side of a single circulation spine, with the closed rooms gathered at the core end where
the plate is interrupted anyway.

Three options were planned on the same shell, which is the useful part of this design —
the differences are the decisions, not the drawing.

## Three layouts at a glance

| | Option 01 | Option 02 | Option 03 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workstation bands | Two, standard pitch | Two, tightened | Two, tightest pitch |
| Cafeteria seats | None — pantry and a short dining run | 45 | 23 |
| Boardroom | 16-seat | 16-seat | 16-seat |
| Enclosed meeting rooms | Four-seat + eight-seat | Same | Same |
| Cabins | MD suite, two directors, shared HR room | Same | Same |
| Client lounge | Private lounge and dining for four | Same | Same |
| Call booths | Two | Two | Two |
| What it optimises for | Comfort and circulation | Staff amenity | Desk count |
| What it gives up | Desk count | Some desk pitch | Amenity space |

The workstation numbers are worth stating carefully. Our layout counts
**68 desks in the upper band and 102 in the lower — 170 in total**. The source layouts
that sit behind options 02 and 03 annotate their workstation zones at **190 to 204**.
Both are true: they are different assumptions about pitch and about how much of the plate
is conceded to circulation and services.

## How the seat count changes the plan

**The circulation spine is the first lever, and the one to protect.** The plan holds a
**2,700 mm central gallery** and marks it *keep clear*. That is the width that lets two
people pass a seated row without anyone moving, and it is also the route trolleys, fit-out
material and eventually furniture take. Narrowing it is the fastest way to add desks and
the fastest way to make a floor feel mean. If the gallery has to give, it should give
locally — at a pinch point — not along its whole length.

**Desks are 1,200 × 600 mm, with 900 mm behind each for the chair.** The reference plan
draws that chair zone as a dotted reservation rather than leaving it implied. This matters
because a desk grid drawn without chair reservations always looks like it fits and never
does, and the error only shows up when the furniture arrives.

**Service zones must be excluded from the desk count.** One area on the plan is marked
*MEP reserved — no desks counted*. Excluding it is the honest thing to do: the electrical
room, the shafts and the equipment runs are not negotiable, and a seat number that
quietly includes them is a number that will fall by the time the services layout is real.
This is the single most common reason a headline capacity figure turns out to be wrong.

**The meeting-room ratio drifts as density rises.** The closed rooms stay the same across
all three options — a 16-seat boardroom, an eight-seat room, a four-seat room and the
client lounge — while the desks go up. At 170 desks that is a comfortable provision. At
204 it is tight, and the two call booths start doing real work, absorbing the one-to-one
and video calls that would otherwise occupy a six-person room.

**Closed rooms belong where the plate is already interrupted.** Here the cabins, boardroom
and lounge collect around the core, off a **1,200 mm cross hall**. That keeps the window
line available to the open floor, so daylight reaches the desks rather than being captured
by a handful of offices — which in turn affects how the lighting and the glazing
performance need to be specified.

## Finishes, and the logic behind them

| Area | Specified as | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open floor | Carpet tile, main grey | Acoustic absorption underfoot, and tiles can be lifted for floor boxes |
| Circulation | Carpet tile, darker grey | Marks the route visually and wears where footfall is heaviest |
| Accents | Carpet tile, blue and indigo | Zoning without partitions |
| Reception and lift lobby | Italian stone | The arrival surface takes the most traffic and is the first thing a visitor reads |
| MD cabin | Wood-look laminate flooring | Warmer register for the one room that is also a meeting space |
| Cafe and pantry | Easy-clean resilient finish | Specified for spills and daily cleaning, not for appearance |

Two details carry more weight than they look. **All four carpet tiles come from one
range**, so backing thickness and pile height match at every junction — mixing ranges to
chase a colour is how you end up with a visible step between two tiles. And the
**stone stops where the carpet starts, at a threshold the drawings locate deliberately**;
left to site, that junction lands wherever the tiler reaches at the end of a day.

## What to settle before construction drawings start

- **The headcount you are actually planning for**, including growth. A plan optimised for
  today's number and a plan with 15% headroom are different plans, not the same plan with
  spare desks.
- **How many closed rooms you genuinely need.** Every cabin is desks, daylight and
  services. The honest test is how often the room is occupied, not who it belongs to.
- **Whether the cafeteria is amenity or overflow seating.** If people will work from it,
  it needs power, acoustic treatment and better lighting than a canteen.
- **Services routes.** This design still carries two open notes — circulation to coordinate,
  and wet services to confirm. Both are normal at concept stage and both must close before
  drawings go out, because a moved shaft moves partitions.
- **Partition strategy against lease term.** Demountable partitions cost more up front and
  can be relocated or removed; drywall is cheaper and permanent. The lease length usually
  decides this, not the budget.
- **What the base building gives you.** Corridor widths, the glazing specification and the
  services capacity are fixed by the building. Design starts from them.
