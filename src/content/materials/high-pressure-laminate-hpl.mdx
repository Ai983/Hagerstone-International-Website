## What it is

**Compact laminate** — layers of kraft paper impregnated with thermosetting
resin, pressed under high heat and pressure into a dense, homogeneous sheet
with a decorative surface. Exterior grades add a double-hardened acrylic
surface layer for UV resistance.

It is specified for rainscreen cladding where a wide range of colours, wood
reproductions and stone reproductions is wanted at a lower weight than stone.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | EN 438-6 for exterior compact grades |
| Thickness | 6mm — 10mm for facade panels |
| Surface | Double-hardened acrylic, UV stabilised |
| Fire class | Standard is combustible; FR grades to B-s1,d0 |
| Fixing | Visible rivet, or rear undercut anchor on rails |
| Density | Approximately 1,350 kg/m³ |

## Why it gets specified

**Colour and pattern range.** Wood and stone reproductions that hold their
appearance far better than paint, at a fraction of the weight of the material
being reproduced.

**Dimensional stability in the panel plane** compared with timber, and no
corrosion path, which suits coastal and high-humidity sites where metal finish
selection becomes restrictive.

**Workability.** It cuts and routs cleanly on standard joinery machinery,
which shortens lead times on bespoke panel sizes.

## The movement problem, stated plainly

HPL expands and contracts measurably with temperature and humidity — more
across the sheet width than along its length. Every facade detail follows from
this:

- Fixing holes are **oversized**, with one fixed point per panel and the rest
  sliding
- Panel joints are **open, with a defined gap**, never butted tight
- Sub-frame rails must allow the panel to move without binding

Ignore this and the panel bows, the fixings elongate the holes, or the sheet
cracks from a corner.

## What drives the cost

Thickness · exterior versus interior grade · fire-retardant grade · decor
(plain colours cost less than wood and stone reproductions) · visible versus
invisible fixing, with undercut anchors adding both material and labour ·
panel size and cutting waste · sub-frame design.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Interior grade used outside.** It fades within a season or two of Indian
  sun. The grade must be named in the specification.
- **Fire grade left unstated.** "HPL" says nothing about combustibility.
- **Fixings pinned tight.** The single most common HPL failure, and entirely
  avoidable at detailing stage.
- **Dark colours on a west elevation.** Surface temperatures climb, movement
  increases, and the joint gaps must be sized for it.

## Standards referenced

Sheet specification and exterior classes under **EN 438**. Reaction-to-fire
classification to **EN 13501-1**. External wall assembly fire requirements
under **NBC 2016, Part 4**.
