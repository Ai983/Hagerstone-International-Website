## What it is

The **perimeter bar that separates the two panes** of an insulated glass unit,
holds the desiccant that keeps the cavity dry, and carries the seals that keep
moisture out.

It is the component that decides how long a double-glazed unit lasts.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Material | Stainless, thermoplastic, or composite |
| Conductivity | Low; "warm edge" class stated |
| Desiccant | Molecular sieve within the spacer |
| Primary seal | Polyisobutylene, the vapour barrier |
| Secondary seal | Silicone or polysulphide, the structural bond |
| Testing | EN 1279 or ASTM E2190 |

## The two seals do different jobs

This is the detail that matters and is routinely misunderstood:

- The **primary seal** (PIB) is the moisture barrier. It stops water vapour
  entering the cavity.
- The **secondary seal** holds the two panes together structurally and protects
  the primary seal.

For structurally glazed facades the secondary seal must be **silicone**,
because polysulphide is not UV stable and structural glazing exposes the edge
seal to sunlight. Using a polysulphide unit in a structurally glazed facade is
a known, repeated and expensive failure.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Aluminium spacers in air-conditioned buildings**, then condensation
  complaints that get blamed on the AC.
- **Polysulphide secondary seal** in a structurally glazed or exposed-edge
  application.
- **Undrained glazing rebate.** Standing water attacks the secondary seal and
  the unit fogs in a few years.
- **Spacer unspecified**, so the cheapest option is supplied silently.
- **Units stored flat or in sun** on site before installation, stressing the
  seal before the building is even complete.

## Standards referenced

Insulating glass unit durability testing under **EN 1279** and **ASTM E2190**.
Fenestration U-value targets under **ECBC 2017**.
