## What it is

**Low-alloy steel** containing copper, chromium, nickel and phosphorus, which
forms a dense oxide layer that adheres to the surface and slows further
corrosion — instead of the flaking rust of ordinary steel.

It is specified for its appearance: a deep orange-brown that changes with
weather and age.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | IS 11587, or ASTM A588 |
| Thickness | 3mm — 8mm for panels and screens |
| Finish | Natural weathered, or accelerated patina |
| Fixing | Stainless or matching weathering steel |
| Drainage | Free-draining; no water traps anywhere |
| Fire class | Non-combustible |

## The conditions it needs

Weathering steel works only where the surface goes through genuine **wet then
fully dry** cycles. That rules out:

- Permanently shaded or sheltered positions
- Details that trap or pond water
- Buried or submerged elements
- Coastal and chloride-laden atmospheres
- Continuously humid enclosed spaces

Where those conditions apply, it simply keeps corroding.

## Designing for run-off

The staining is the practical problem on most projects. Plan for it:

- Drip details that throw water clear of surfaces below
- Avoid light-coloured stone, concrete or paving directly beneath
- Protect or plan to clean adjacent surfaces during the stabilising period
- Consider factory pre-patinated material where the programme cannot absorb it

## Where projects go wrong

- **Coastal specification.** The patina never stabilises.
- **No run-off strategy**, so the plaza below is permanently stained.
- **Water-trapping details** — flat horizontal ledges, closed sections with no
  drainage.
- **Carbon steel fixings**, which corrode at a different rate and streak.
- **Contact with other metals** without isolation, setting up galvanic
  corrosion.

## Standards referenced

Weather-resistant structural steel under **IS 11587** and **ASTM A588**. Wind
loading on cladding under **IS 875 (Part 3)**.
