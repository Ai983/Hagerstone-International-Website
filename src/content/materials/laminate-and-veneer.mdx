## What it is

Two different finishing materials that get specified together:

- **Decorative laminate** — resin-impregnated papers pressed onto a decorative
  surface layer, bonded to a substrate
- **Natural veneer** — thin slices of real timber, bonded and then finished on
  site or in the factory

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Laminate standard | IS 2046, grade and thickness stated |
| Laminate thickness | 0.8mm — 1.5mm for horizontal surfaces |
| Veneer thickness | 0.5mm — 0.6mm typical |
| Substrate | Plywood to IS 303, MDF, or particle board |
| Balancing | **Required on the reverse face** |
| Fire class | Named EN 13501-1 class where required |

## Substrate matters as much as the face

| Substrate | Best for | Watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BWP / marine plywood | Wet areas, high humidity | Cost |
| Commercial plywood | General joinery | Voids and quality variation |
| MDF | Painted and routed profiles | Poor in moisture |
| Particle board | Cost-driven carcass work | Weak screw holding |

## Veneer matching

Specify the matching intent, because it must be ordered that way:

**Book match** mirrors alternate leaves · **slip match** repeats the same
orientation · **random match** deliberately mixes for a rustic look ·
**sequence match** keeps a run continuous across several panels.

All of these require the flitch to be reserved and kept in order. None can be
recovered later.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Unbalanced panels**, which bow and twist.
- **Veneer ordered piecemeal**, so matching across a wall is impossible.
- **MDF in humid areas**, swelling at the edges.
- **Fire class ignored** on corridor and escape route panelling.
- **Site-finished veneer in a dusty shell**, which never achieves a clean
  finish.

## Standards referenced

Decorative laminate under **IS 2046**. Plywood under **IS 303**.
Reaction-to-fire classification to **EN 13501-1**.
