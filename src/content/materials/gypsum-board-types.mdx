## What it is

A **gypsum core faced with paper liner**, produced in grades that vary the core
additives and the liner to suit different duties.

## The grades

| Grade | Identifying colour (typical) | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular | Ivory / grey liner | General partitions and ceilings |
| Moisture resistant | Green | Pantries, humid areas, wet-area backing |
| Fire resistant | Pink / red | Within tested fire-rated systems |
| Impact resistant | Varies | Corridors, schools, high-traffic areas |
| Acoustic / high density | Varies | Where partition sound rating matters |

Colours vary by manufacturer and should never be the basis of specification —
the grade and standard reference should be.

## The system, not the board

A partition's fire rating, acoustic rating and structural performance are
properties of the **complete tested assembly**:

- Board type, thickness and number of layers
- Stud gauge, depth and centres
- Insulation type and density in the cavity
- Screw type, length and spacing pattern
- Joint staggering between layers
- Head, base and junction detailing

Substituting any one of these breaks the test. On a fire-rated wall that is not
a paperwork issue — it is the wall not doing what the fire strategy assumes.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Fire board in an untested arrangement**, assumed to give a rating.
- **Moisture resistant board inside showers**, where cement board is required.
- **Screw spacing widened** to save time, changing the tested system.
- **Joints not staggered** between layers in double-layer systems.
- **Boards installed before the building is dry**, so they absorb moisture and
  sag.

## Standards referenced

Gypsum plaster boards under **IS 2095 (Part 1)** and **EN 520**. Fire and life
safety provisions under **NBC 2016, Part 4**.
