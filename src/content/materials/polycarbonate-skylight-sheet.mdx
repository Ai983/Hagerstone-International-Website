## What it is

Translucent **polycarbonate sheet**, profiled to match the metal roof sheet
around it, used to bring daylight into a large industrial or warehouse space
without electric lighting.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Type | Solid profiled, or multiwall |
| Thickness | 1.5mm — 3mm profiled; 6mm — 25mm multiwall |
| UV protection | Co-extruded layer, one or both faces |
| Light transmission | Specified as a percentage |
| Profile | Matched to the adjacent metal sheet |
| Fire class | Named EN 13501-1 class |

## Solid profiled against multiwall

| | Solid profiled | Multiwall |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insulation | Poor | Better, sealed air cells |
| Light quality | Clear, higher transmission | Diffused, softer |
| Strength | Good impact resistance | Good, lighter |
| Detailing | Laps like the metal sheet | Needs sealed edge tapes |
| Use | Standard industrial roof lights | Conditioned or diffused-light spaces |

## The daylight and heat trade-off

Roof lights cut lighting load — and that saving works against the ECBC lighting
power density target — but they also admit solar heat directly onto the working
floor. In an air-conditioned building that heat becomes cooling load.

The practical answer on most Indian industrial roofs is a **modest percentage
of roof area**, distributed evenly for uniform light rather than concentrated,
with diffusing multiwall used where glare on a working plane matters.

## Where projects go wrong

- **UV face installed downward.** The single most common and most damaging
  error.
- **Too much skylight area**, turning a lighting saving into a cooling cost.
- **Fixings through the sheet without oversized holes.** Polycarbonate expands
  considerably more than steel; pinned sheets crack around the screws.
- **Edges of multiwall left unsealed**, so dust and moisture enter the cells and
  the sheet clouds permanently.
- **Walking on roof lights.** They are not a walking surface; many industrial
  roof falls happen through them.

## Standards referenced

Skylight and daylighting provisions under **ECBC 2017**. Wind loading under
**IS 875 (Part 3)**. Reaction-to-fire classification to **EN 13501-1**.
