  - question: "Why is engineered wood used instead of solid wood?"
    answer: "Dimensional stability. Solid wood moves substantially across the grain with changes in humidity, and Indian conditions swing between monsoon humidity and dry winter air. Engineered boards have a cross-laminated core that restrains that movement, so gaps and cupping are far less pronounced. They can also be laid over underfloor heating and on concrete, where solid wood is problematic."
  - question: "What wear layer thickness should be specified?"
    answer: "The wear layer is the solid timber top, and it determines how many times the floor can be sanded and refinished. Roughly 0.6mm is a veneer that cannot be sanded at all, 2 to 3mm allows one light refinish, and 4 to 6mm allows several over a long life. For a commercial installation intended to last, 4mm and above is the meaningful specification and it should be stated in millimetres."
  - question: "What subfloor moisture is acceptable?"

The system's stated moisture limit, a moisture barrier where required, and an acclimatisation period for the boards in the finished space are all part of the specification."

## What it is

A **multi-layer board** with a solid timber wear layer bonded to a
cross-laminated plywood or HDF core, giving the appearance of solid wood with
much better dimensional stability.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | EN 13489, characteristics to EN 14342 |
| Wear layer | 0.6mm veneer — 6mm solid; **state it** |
| Core | Birch or hardwood plywood, or HDF |
| Total thickness | 10mm — 20mm |
| Finish | UV-cured lacquer, oil, or brushed and oiled |
| Installation | Floating, glued, or secret nailed |

## What decides service life

**Wear layer thickness.** Everything else is secondary. A 0.6mm veneer looks
identical on day one and cannot be sanded when it wears; a 4mm layer survives
several refinishes across decades.

**Finish system.** Lacquer resists spills and is lower maintenance but is hard
to repair locally. Oil marks more easily but can be spot-repaired without
refinishing the whole floor — which matters in a large office.

## Indian conditions specifically

Humidity swings here are wide. Boards must be **acclimatised** in the finished,
air-conditioned space before laying — not in a hot open shell — and expansion
gaps at every perimeter and threshold are not optional. A floor laid tight to
walls in dry winter conditions will buckle in the first monsoon.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Wear layer unspecified**, so a thin veneer arrives.
- **Laid on a damp slab.** Cupping and lifting follow.
- **No acclimatisation**, or acclimatised in the wrong conditions.
- **Expansion gaps omitted** at perimeters, columns and thresholds.
- **Laid before wet trades finish**, absorbing construction moisture.

## Standards referenced

Multi-layer parquet elements under **EN 13489**. Wood flooring characteristics
under **EN 14342**. Concrete subfloor requirements under **IS 456:2000**.
