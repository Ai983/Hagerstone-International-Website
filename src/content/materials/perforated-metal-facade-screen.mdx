    answer: "They reduce it compared with a solid panel of the same size, but they do not eliminate it, and the reduction is not simply proportional to open area. ## What it is

Sheet metal — usually aluminium, sometimes stainless or weathering steel —
**punched with a repeating pattern of holes** and formed into panels or
cassettes, hung in front of glazing or a solid wall.

It does three jobs at once: shading, screening and appearance.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Material | Aluminium to IS 737, or stainless / Corten |
| Thickness | 2mm — 4mm typical for panels |
| Perforation | Round, square, slot, or bespoke pattern |
| Open area | Stated as a percentage — the key variable |
| Edge margin | Unperforated border for stiffness and fixing |
| Finish | PVDF powder coat or anodised |

## What the screen achieves

**Solar shading without blocking the view.** Because the screen sits in front
of the glass with a gap, it intercepts direct sun before it reaches the
glazing, while occupants still see out through the perforations.

**Glare control** on east and west elevations where low sun defeats overhangs.

**Privacy** at close-facing elevations, working in one direction because of the
brightness difference between inside and outside.

**Plant and car park screening**, concealing equipment while allowing the
ventilation those spaces need.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Open area chosen visually**, not against sun path and daylight needs.
- **Wind load assumed negligible** because the panel is perforated.
- **No unperforated margin**, so the panel has no stiffness at its edges and
  distorts at the fixings.
- **Panels rotated on site** where the perforation pattern or image has a
  defined orientation.
- **Cleaning and maintenance access not considered.** A screen in front of glass
  makes the glass much harder to clean, and that has to be planned.

## Standards referenced

Aluminium sheet under **IS 737**. Shading and SHGC provisions under
**ECBC 2017**. Wind loading on cladding and screens under **IS 875 (Part 3)**.
