## What it is

**Mineral fibre spun from molten rock**, bound into slabs, rolls or loose fill.
It insulates thermally, absorbs sound, and does not burn.

That last property is why it appears in positions where nothing else is
acceptable.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | IS 8183, bonded mineral wool |
| Thermal conductivity | Approximately 0.035 — 0.040 W/mK |
| Fire class | A1, non-combustible, to EN 13501-1 |
| Melting point | Above 1,000°C |
| Density range | 48 — 150 kg/m³ by application |
| Facing | Plain, tissue faced, or foil faced |

## Density by application

| Application | Density |
| --- | --- |
| General thermal infill | 48 — 64 kg/m³ |
| Ventilated facade slab | 80 — 100 kg/m³ |
| Cavity barriers | 100 — 150 kg/m³ |
| Acoustic partition infill | 48 — 80 kg/m³ |
| Roof deck board | 140 — 160 kg/m³ |

## Where it is the only correct answer

- **Cavity barriers** at every compartment line in a ventilated facade
- **Insulation in a ventilated cavity** on high-rise and high-occupancy
  buildings
- **Fire-rated partitions and shafts**, where the tested system names it
- **Acoustic separation** between tenancies and around plant rooms
- **High-temperature plant insulation**, where organic foams cannot go

## Thermal trade-off, stated honestly

Rock wool insulates less well per millimetre than PIR — roughly 0.037 against
0.022 W/mK — so reaching the same U-value needs a thicker build-up. On
envelopes where depth is constrained, that is a real cost. Many industrial
projects therefore run PIR across general areas and rock wool at fire-critical
lines, which is a legitimate and common specification.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Density unspecified.** The single most common error.
- **Facade slabs too soft**, so they slump in the cavity over time and leave
  gaps at the top of each storey.
- **Cavity barriers substituted** for a cheaper general-purpose slab.
- **Left exposed to monsoon rain** before the outer skin is fixed.
- **Compressed to fit.** Compressing the slab reduces its thermal performance.

## Standards referenced

Bonded mineral wool requirements under **IS 8183**. Reaction-to-fire
classification to **EN 13501-1**. Opaque assembly U-value under **ECBC 2017**.
