## What it is

Float glass with **metal oxide colourants added to the melt**, giving bronze,
grey, green, blue or black through the full thickness of the pane.

Because the colour is in the body, it cannot scratch, peel or wear.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | IS 14900 for float glass |
| Colours | Bronze, grey, green, blue, black |
| Thickness | 5mm — 12mm common |
| Mechanism | Absorbs solar energy; does not reflect it |
| Heat treatment | Heat-strengthened advisable on sunny elevations |
| Combination | Frequently used as substrate for a coating |

## How tint behaves, honestly

Absorbed energy has to go somewhere. Roughly, a tinted pane re-radiates part of
the absorbed heat inward and part outward, so the net solar reduction is far
smaller than the drop in visible light suggests. A building can end up darker
inside without being much cooler — which then increases artificial lighting
load and works against the ECBC lighting power density target.

That trade-off is the reason tint alone is rarely the right answer in Indian
conditions, and why spectrally selective coatings on a lightly tinted or clear
substrate have largely replaced heavy tints on commercial work.

## Where it still makes sense

Glare control on elevations where a view must be retained · architectural
colour where a specific elevation tone is wanted · as a **substrate** beneath a
solar control coating · secondary and back-of-house glazing where coated glass
is not justified.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Dark tint in annealed glass** on a sunny elevation — thermal stress cracks.
- **Partial shading from a reveal or louvre** across a tinted pane, which
  concentrates the stress at the shadow line.
- **Relying on tint for an SHGC target.**
- **Mixing float batches**, since tint colour varies subtly between runs and
  shows across a continuous elevation.

## Standards referenced

Float glass specification under **IS 14900**. Fenestration SHGC under
**ECBC 2017**. Architectural safety glass to **IS 2553 (Part 1)**.
