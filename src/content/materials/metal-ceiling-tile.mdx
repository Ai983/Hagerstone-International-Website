  - question: "Do perforations alone make a ceiling acoustic?"
    answer: "No. The perforations let sound through the metal, but the absorption happens in the acoustic fleece or mineral wool pad behind it. A perforated tile with nothing behind it reflects almost as much sound as a solid one. The specification must name the backing — fleece type or pad density — alongside the perforation pattern, or the ceiling looks acoustic and performs as though it is not."
  - question: "Which system should be used — lay-in, clip-in or hook-on?"
    answer: "Lay-in sits in an exposed grid and is cheapest and easiest to access. Clip-in gives a flush monolithic appearance with concealed grid but is harder to demount. Hook-on allows individual tiles to be lowered on a hinge for access without disturbing neighbours, which suits corridors dense with services. The choice follows from how often the void needs access, not from appearance alone."

## What it is

**Pressed aluminium or steel tiles** suspended on a grid, plain or perforated,
with an acoustic backing where absorption is required.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Material | Aluminium 0.5 — 0.7mm, or steel 0.5 — 0.6mm |
| System | Lay-in, clip-in, hook-on, or linear / baffle |
| Perforation | Pattern and open area stated |
| Acoustic backing | Fleece or mineral pad — **specify it** |
| Absorption | αw rating to EN ISO 11654 |
| Finish | Powder coat, RAL or custom |
| Standard | EN 13964 for the suspended system |

## The acoustic chain

For a metal ceiling to absorb sound, all three must be specified together:

1. **Perforation** — open area sufficient to let sound through the sheet
2. **Backing** — fleece bonded to the tile, or a mineral wool pad above it
3. **Plenum** — the void depth behind, which affects low-frequency absorption

Name the **αw value** required and let the supplier demonstrate the build-up
that achieves it, rather than specifying a perforation pattern and hoping.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Perforated tiles with no backing**, giving the look without the performance.
- **Grid not levelled** before tiles go in; metal shows every deviation.
- **Handled without gloves.** Powder-coated metal marks and the marks stay.
- **Access strategy not matched to the system**, so a clip-in ceiling has to be
  partly dismantled every time a valve needs reaching.
- **Seismic and hanger requirements ignored** on larger installations, where
  EN 13964 sets the framework.

## Standards referenced

Suspended ceiling requirements under **EN 13964**. Sound absorption rating
under **EN ISO 11654**. Reaction-to-fire classification to **EN 13501-1**.
