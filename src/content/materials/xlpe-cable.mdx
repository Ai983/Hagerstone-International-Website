## What it is

Power cable with **cross-linked polyethylene insulation**. The cross-linking
turns a thermoplastic into a thermoset, so the insulation does not soften at
the temperatures that limit PVC.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | IS 7098, part by voltage grade |
| Conductor | Copper or aluminium, class stated |
| Continuous rating | 90°C conductor temperature |
| Short circuit | 250°C for a short duration |
| Armour | Steel wire, or aluminium strip on single core |
| Sheath | PVC standard; FRLS where required |

## Derating is the design, not the catalogue

A cable's published rating assumes reference conditions. Real installations
rarely match them, and every departure reduces capacity:

| Factor | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| High ambient temperature | Reduces rating |
| Grouping in a tray or conduit | Reduces rating, significantly when bunched |
| Installation method | Enclosed runs derate more than free air |
| Soil thermal resistivity | Governs buried cable capacity |

Applying these under IS 3961 and IS 732 is what separates a cable that runs
warm from one that runs hot for twenty years and fails early.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Sizing from the catalogue rating** with no derating applied.
- **Bunched cables in a hot shaft**, where the combined grouping and ambient
  factors are severe.
- **Bending radius ignored** during pulling, damaging the insulation invisibly.
- **Single-core cables with steel wire armour**, inducing circulating currents.
- **Gland and armour termination done badly**, so the armour earth path is not
  continuous — an electrical safety defect, not a finish issue.

## Standards referenced

Cable construction under **IS 7098**. Installation practice under **IS 732**.
Current ratings and derating factors under **IS 3961**.
