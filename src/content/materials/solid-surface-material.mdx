## What it is

A **homogeneous composite** of acrylic or polyester resin with mineral filler,
supplied in sheet form and fabricated into counters, desks, vanities and wall
linings.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Composition | Acrylic (PMMA) or polyester resin with ATH filler |
| Standard | ISO 19712 |
| Thickness | 6mm, 12mm typical |
| Joints | Colour-matched adhesive, sanded seamless |
| Thermoforming | Possible; radius limits by product |
| Fire class | Named EN 13501-1 class |

## Where it earns its cost

**Seamless runs.** A twelve-metre reception counter with no visible joint is
something only this material family does well.

**Curved and formed shapes.** Heating the sheet allows genuine three-dimensional
forms — curved reception fronts, coved upstands, integrated bowls — without
joints.

**Hygiene.** Non-porous with no seams, which is why it is standard in
healthcare, laboratories and food preparation counters.

**Repairability.** Damage is sanded out rather than replaced.

## Fabrication requirements

It must be fabricated by a trained fabricator with the right tooling. Inside
corners need a **radius**, never a sharp internal angle, because sharp corners
concentrate stress and crack — this is the most common solid surface failure
and it is entirely a fabrication decision. Cut-outs for sinks and sockets need
the same treatment.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Sharp internal corners** at cut-outs, cracking within months.
- **Inadequate substrate support**, allowing slow sag in warm conditions.
- **Direct heat** without protection.
- **Fabricated by a general joiner** without the adhesive system or training.
- **Colour batch mixing**, which shows at seams meant to be invisible.

## Standards referenced

Solid surfacing materials under **ISO 19712**. Related surface performance
testing in the **EN 438** family. Reaction-to-fire classification to
**EN 13501-1**.
