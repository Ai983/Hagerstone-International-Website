## What it is

A **steel channel section cast into the concrete slab edge or column** before
the pour, with a slot that accepts a special bolt head. Facade brackets bolt to
it and slide along it for adjustment.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Material | Hot dip galvanised steel, or stainless |
| Profile | Plain or serrated, by longitudinal load |
| Anchors | Welded studs into the concrete |
| Design basis | EN 1992-4 or equivalent |
| Adjustment | Free along length; limited across |
| Bolts | Matched system bolts only |

## Why it suits facades

A curtain wall or cladding bracket must land within a few millimetres of where
the drawing says, on a concrete frame that was built to construction
tolerances. Those two facts fight each other.

Cast-in channels resolve it: the **bracket slides horizontally** to wherever it
is actually needed, and the load path into the concrete is fixed and tested
rather than discovered by drilling.

They also remove drilling noise, dust and the risk of cutting reinforcement on
an occupied or finished structure.

## The sequencing requirement

This is the practical catch. The channel is set **before concrete is poured**,
which means the facade design must be far enough advanced at structure stage to
know where brackets go. On projects where the facade package is procured late,
the information does not exist in time and the team falls back to post-drilled
anchors.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Facade design not advanced enough** at pour stage, so channels are guessed
  or omitted.
- **Channels displaced during pouring**, which is why they need fixing properly
  to formwork and surveying afterwards.
- **Non-system bolts used.** The channel's capacity depends on the matched bolt
  head; ordinary bolts do not engage correctly.
- **Filler strip not removed**, leaving the channel full of concrete.
- **Longitudinal load ignored** on a plain channel that cannot take it.

## Standards referenced

Concrete design under **IS 456:2000**. Fastenings in concrete under
**EN 1992-4**. Wind loading transferred through the brackets under
**IS 875 (Part 3)**.
