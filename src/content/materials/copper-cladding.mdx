## What it is

**Rolled copper sheet**, formed into standing seam, shingle or cassette
cladding. It oxidises through a sequence of colours over years and is among the
longest-lasting building metals in service.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | EN 1172 |
| Thickness | 0.6mm — 0.8mm typical |
| Finish | Mill bright, pre-oxidised brown, pre-patinated green |
| System | Standing seam, shingle, cassette |
| Fixing | Copper or stainless clips only |
| Fire class | A1, non-combustible |

## The patina sequence

| Stage | Appearance | Rough timescale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New | Bright salmon-pink | At installation |
| Early | Darkening brown | Months |
| Mature | Deep brown to near black | Years |
| Green patina | Green carbonate | Decades, climate dependent |

The green stage needs sustained humidity and atmospheric exposure. In dry
inland Indian conditions it may never develop evenly, which is why
pre-patinated sheet exists.

## Detailing rules

- **Nothing vulnerable downstream.** No zinc, aluminium or galvanised steel
  anywhere copper run-off can reach.
- **Copper or stainless fixings only.** Any other metal in contact corrodes.
- **Ventilated, drained backing**, as with all metal rainscreens.
- **Clips that allow movement**, since copper expands significantly.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Copper draining onto aluminium windows** — a classic and expensive error.
- **Client expecting green on handover**, when it is decades away.
- **Steel fixings** in contact with the sheet.
- **Uneven weathering** across an elevation because rain reaches some areas and
  not others; it is a real appearance risk that detailing can mitigate.
- **Accessible low-level copper** in locations where theft is a live risk.

## Standards referenced

Copper sheet and strip for building under **EN 1172**. Reaction-to-fire
classification to **EN 13501-1**. Wind loading under **IS 875 (Part 3)**.
