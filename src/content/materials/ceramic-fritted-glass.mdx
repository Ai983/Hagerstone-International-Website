## What it is

Glass printed with a **ceramic ink** that is then fired into the surface during
toughening. The ink fuses with the glass, so the pattern is permanent and
integral rather than applied.

Patterns range from fine dots and lines to full opacity.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Application | Screen printed, then fired at toughening |
| Coverage | 10% — 100%, specified as a percentage |
| Pattern | Dots, lines, bespoke image, or solid |
| Surface | Usually surface 2 of a DGU for protection |
| Glass | Toughened or heat-strengthened, to IS 2553 Pt 1 |
| Durability | Permanent; fused into the glass |

## What frit is used for

**Solar control without a shading device.** Coverage percentage becomes a
design variable you can tune per elevation — denser on west, lighter on north.
Useful for reaching an ECBC SHGC target when the coating alone is not enough.

**Spandrel treatment.** Opaque frit conceals slab edges, insulation and
shadow boxes while keeping the elevation reading as continuous glass.

**Bird safety and visual manifestation.** A patterned surface makes large glass
areas visible, which matters on full-height glazing and at entrances.

**Privacy grading.** Density can be varied up a facade so lower floors are more
screened than upper ones.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Frit specified without a coverage percentage.** "Fritted glass" is not a
  specification; 30% and 70% behave completely differently.
- **Pattern direction not set out.** A linear frit has an orientation, and
  panels rotated on site show it immediately.
- **Spandrel frit on annealed glass.** Thermal stress behind an opaque panel
  causes breakage; heat-strengthened is the minimum.
- **Frit on surface 1** where it is exposed to weather and cleaning abrasion
  unnecessarily.

## Standards referenced

Architectural safety glass to **IS 2553 (Part 1)**. Fenestration SHGC targets
by climate zone under **ECBC 2017**. Coated glass requirements under
**EN 1096**.
