  - question: "How does an undercut anchor differ from an expansion anchor?"
    answer: "An expansion anchor works by pressing outward against the hole, which puts the surrounding material in tension — acceptable in concrete, dangerous in a thin stone slab where it can split the panel. An undercut anchor sits in a conically widened hole and grips the shoulder of that cone by form rather than by pressure, so it develops its capacity without inducing splitting stress in the stone."
  - question: "Why is drilling accuracy so critical?"
    answer: "The anchor relies on the geometry of the undercut cone. If the hole is too deep, too shallow, off-angle or the cone is incompletely formed, the anchor either does not engage the shoulder properly or sits too close to the face and blows out a cone of stone under load. Purpose-made drilling rigs with depth stops exist precisely because hand drilling cannot hold that tolerance reliably."

## What it is

A **rear-fixed anchor** set into a conically undercut hole drilled part-way
into the back of a stone or porcelain panel. The anchor grips the shoulder of
the cone mechanically, with no expansion force against the panel.

Nothing shows on the face of the panel.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Anchor material | Stainless, grade by exposure |
| Hole | Conical undercut, purpose-drilled |
| Depth | Part-way through; never breaking the face |
| Panel thickness | Minimum set by anchor and stone strength |
| Testing | Pull-out to ASTM C1354 on the supplied batch |
| Edge distance | Governed by the system; verified per panel size |

## Why it replaced kerf fixing

Kerf fixing cuts a slot into the panel edge and slides a clip into it. It
works, but it relies on the strength of the slab edge, which is the weakest
part of the panel and the part most likely to have been chipped in handling.

Undercut anchors move the load path to the **back** of the panel, away from the
edges. That allows thinner panels, larger formats and cleaner joints, and it
removes the edge as the failure point.

## The quality chain

| Step | What must be right |
| --- | --- |
| Stone selection | Tested flexural and compressive values, this batch |
| Hole drilling | Depth, angle and cone geometry within tolerance |
| Anchor setting | Correct torque, fully engaged |
| Testing | Pull-out on production panels, not just samples |
| Installation | Each panel independently supported |

## Where projects go wrong

- **Hand drilling** instead of a rigged drill with a depth stop.
- **Test values borrowed** from another project or from quarry literature.
- **Panels too thin** for the anchor system chosen.
- **Edge distances reduced** to suit a panel layout, without recalculation.
- **304 stainless anchors in coastal exposure**, where 316 is required.

## Standards referenced

Stone anchorage strength testing to **ASTM C1354**. Stone identification under
**IS 1123**. Wind loading the anchorage must resist under **IS 875 (Part 3)**.
