## What it is

**Galvanised steel sheet ductwork**, fabricated into rectangular or round
sections to distribute conditioned air. The default air distribution method in
Indian commercial buildings.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Material | Galvanised steel to IS 277, coating stated |
| Gauge | By duct size and pressure class per SMACNA |
| Joints | Slip and drive, or flanged systems |
| Stiffeners | Spacing by size and pressure |
| Sealing | Sealant or gasket at every joint |
| Leakage class | Stated and tested on higher-pressure systems |

## What the specification must state

- **Coating mass** of the galvanised sheet, not just "GI"
- **Gauge table** referenced, by size and pressure class
- **Joint system**, since flanged systems seal better than slip and drive
- **Sealant type** and where it is applied
- **Leakage class** and whether testing is required
- **Insulation** type, thickness and facing, separately

## Where the money is lost

Duct leakage is invisible and expensive. Air lost through unsealed transverse
joints, unsealed longitudinal seams and poorly made take-offs is air the fan
moved and the chiller cooled for nothing. On a fit-out where the tenant pays
the running cost for a decade, sealing is the highest-return detail in the
whole HVAC package.

## Where projects go wrong

- **One gauge used throughout**, regardless of duct size.
- **Joints unsealed** because the ceiling is going up and nobody will see them.
- **Flexible duct used for long runs** instead of short final connections,
  adding large pressure losses.
- **Stiffeners omitted on large ducts**, so they drum audibly under fan
  pressure.
- **Insulation compressed by hangers**, losing thickness where it is needed.

## Standards referenced

Galvanised sheet under **IS 277**. Duct construction, gauge and sealing per
**SMACNA HVAC Duct Construction Standards**. Duct sealing and insulation
requirements under **ECBC 2017**.
