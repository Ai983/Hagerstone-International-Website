## What it is

**Modular textile floor tiles**, typically 500 × 500mm, laid loose or with
tackifier on a prepared subfloor or a raised access floor.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Pile fibre | Nylon 6 or 6,6; solution dyed preferred |
| Pile weight | 500 — 1,000+ g/m² — **state it** |
| Construction | Loop, cut pile, or cut and loop |
| Backing | Bitumen, PVC, or cushion |
| Use class | EN 1307 / EN ISO 10874 class stated |
| Fire class | Named EN 13501-1 class |

## What actually determines how it looks in three years

**Pile weight and fibre.** Solution-dyed nylon holds colour and resists
cleaning chemicals far better than piece-dyed alternatives.

**Construction.** Loop pile resists crushing in traffic lanes better than cut
pile, which is why corridors and lobbies are usually loop.

**Backing.** A dimensionally stable backing keeps tiles tight at the joints;
a poor one lets tiles shrink and open gaps that collect dirt.

**Laying pattern.** Quarter-turn, monolithic or ashlar — each hides or reveals
shading differently, and the pattern must be specified because it cannot be
changed later.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Pile weight unspecified** and a light grade laid in heavy traffic.
- **Batch mixing.** Tiles from different production batches shade visibly under
  office lighting; batch numbers must match across a floor plate.
- **Laid on a damp or dusty subfloor**, so the tackifier never grips.
- **No attic stock retained.** Matching tiles years later is usually impossible.
- **Wrong laying direction**, which shows as banding once the furniture is out
  and the light rakes across the floor.

## Standards referenced

Pile carpet classification under **EN 1307**. Floor covering use classes under
**EN ISO 10874**. Reaction-to-fire classification to **EN 13501-1**.
