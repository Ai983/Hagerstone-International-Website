## What it is

Glass laminated with an **electrically switchable film**. Two distinct
technologies sit under the label, and they do different jobs.

| | PDLC | Electrochromic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Switches | Clear ↔ opaque | Clear ↔ tinted |
| Speed | Near instant | Minutes |
| Purpose | Privacy | Solar and glare control |
| Typical use | Interior partitions | Exterior facade glazing |
| Opacity | Fully obscuring | Never fully opaque |

## What the specification must cover

Because this is glass **and** an electrical installation, both sides need
defining:

- Panel sizes and the maximum size the film is available in
- **Power supply location** and the concealed cable route to each panel
- Switching method — wall switch, BMS, occupancy sensor, app
- Failure state: what the glass does when power is lost
- Edge detail, since the busbar and wiring must be concealed within the frame
- Safety classification of the laminated build-up to IS 2553 (Part 1)

The wiring is the part that gets missed. A switchable panel needs a cable to it,
and that route has to be designed into the partition head or the floor before
anything is built.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Specified late**, after partitions are designed with no cable route.
- **Power failure state not considered**, so a boardroom goes opaque in an
  outage, or a facade sticks clear during peak sun.
- **Expecting solar control from PDLC.** It does not deliver it.
- **Panel size exceeding film width**, forcing a joint the architect did not
  expect.
- **No isolation or maintenance access** to the driver and transformer.

## Standards referenced

Architectural safety glass to **IS 2553 (Part 1)**. Fenestration provisions
under **ECBC 2017**. Electrical installation practice under **IS 732**.
