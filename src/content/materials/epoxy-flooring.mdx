## What it is

A **resin flooring system** applied to a concrete substrate, ranging from a thin
sealing coat to a thick trowelled screed.

| System | Thickness | Duty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coating | 0.2 — 0.5mm | Dust sealing, light foot traffic |
| Self-levelling | 2 — 3mm | General industrial, forklift traffic |
| Trowelled screed | 4 — 9mm | Heavy impact, chemical, thermal cycling |
| Anti-static | Varies | Electronics, clean rooms |

## Preparation is the specification

The finish is only as good as what it is stuck to. Required, not optional:

- **Mechanical preparation** — shot blasting or diamond grinding to remove
  laitance and open the surface
- **Moisture testing** to the system's stated limit before any resin is applied
- **Crack and joint treatment**, since movement joints must be carried through
  the resin, not covered over
- **Edge and upstand detailing**, including coving where hygiene requires it

Most epoxy failures are preparation failures, and they are visible within
months as peeling, bubbling or delamination in traffic lanes.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Acid etching instead of mechanical preparation.**
- **Applied to a green or damp slab.** Concrete needs its drying time.
- **Movement joints bridged**, so the floor cracks where the slab moves.
- **System thickness below the duty**, wearing through in aisles.
- **Applied in poor ambient conditions**, since cure depends on temperature and
  humidity.
- **Standard epoxy in daylight**, which yellows.
