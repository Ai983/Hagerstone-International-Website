## What it is

**Chlorinated polyvinyl chloride pipe**, solvent-welded, used for hot and cold
potable water distribution. It has largely displaced galvanised iron for
internal plumbing in Indian buildings.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | IS 15778, or ASTM F441 schedules |
| Sizing | SDR 11 common for distribution |
| Max temperature | Approximately 93°C |
| Jointing | Solvent cement, CPVC grade only |
| Sizes | Typically 15mm — 50mm internal distribution |
| Support | Closer spacing than metal; expands more |

## Why it replaced GI internally

**No corrosion and no scaling.** A GI hot water line in hard Indian water
narrows progressively with scale until flow at the far fixtures fails. CPVC
does not scale in the same way, so flow stays as designed.

**No galvanic issues**, no threading, no rust staining at fittings.

**Faster installation** with solvent joints rather than threading and sealing.

## The two things that get it wrong

**Thermal expansion.** CPVC moves considerably more than metal with temperature.
A hot water run fixed rigidly at every clip will bow between supports or stress
the joints. Expansion loops or offsets and correctly spaced clips are part of
the design, not an optional refinement.

**Cure time.** Solvent cement joints reach strength over time, not instantly.
Pressure testing early is the single most common cause of CPVC leaks.

## Where projects go wrong

- **UPVC substituted on hot lines.** It softens and sags.
- **Wrong solvent cement.** UPVC cement on CPVC does not make a sound joint.
- **Support spacing copied from metal pipe practice.**
- **No allowance for expansion** on long hot runs.
- **Pipe exposed to sunlight** during storage or in service; CPVC degrades under
  UV and must be protected or painted where exposed.

## Standards referenced

CPVC pipe for potable water under **IS 15778** and **ASTM F441**. Building
drainage practice under **IS 12251**.
