## What it is

PVC-based resilient flooring, supplied in three broad forms:

| Type | Form | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **LVT** | Planks and tiles | Offices, retail, hospitality |
| **Homogeneous sheet** | Roll, heat welded | Hospitals, labs, clean areas |
| **Heterogeneous sheet** | Roll, layered | Education, general commercial |

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Classification | EN ISO 10874 use class, e.g. 33 for heavy commercial |
| Wear layer | 0.3 / 0.55 / 0.7mm and above — **state it** |
| Total thickness | 2mm — 3mm typical |
| Backing | Dryback, loose lay, or click |
| Fire and slip | Class stated per application |

## What the specification must cover

Use class · wear layer thickness · installation method, since loose lay, dryback
adhesive and click systems behave very differently under rolling loads ·
subfloor moisture limit and test method · welding requirement for sheet · skirting
and coving detail · transition and threshold details.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Wear layer unspecified**, so a thin residential-grade product is laid in a
  commercial corridor.
- **Laid on a damp slab.** Bubbling and lifting appear months later and the
  whole floor comes up.
- **Subfloor not flat.** Every undulation reads through resilient flooring, and
  it cannot be corrected afterwards.
- **Heavy point loads** from cabinets and racking on a thin wear layer,
  indenting permanently.
- **Sheet joints not welded** in wet or hygienic areas, leaving open seams.

## Standards referenced

Resilient floor covering classification under **EN ISO 10874**. PVC floor
covering requirements under **EN 649**. Concrete subfloor requirements under
**IS 456:2000**.
