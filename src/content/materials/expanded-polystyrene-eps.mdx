  - question: "How does EPS compare with XPS?"
    answer: "EPS is made from expanded beads fused together, so it has interstitial voids between beads and absorbs more water than XPS, which is extruded as a continuous closed-cell mass. XPS therefore performs better where moisture is present and under load. EPS is significantly cheaper and, at equal density, thermally close enough for dry above-ground applications where its lower cost wins."
  - question: "Can EPS be used as a sandwich panel core?"
    answer: "It is, widely, in cold rooms and budget industrial panels, because it is cheap and light. Its fire behaviour is the limitation: EPS softens and melts at relatively low temperature and the core is combustible, so in any building where fire performance governs the envelope, PIR or rock wool cored panels are the correct specification and EPS is not."

## What it is

**Expanded polystyrene** — beads expanded with steam and fused into blocks,
then cut into boards or moulded into shapes. The cheapest common insulation and
the most widely misused.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | IS 4671 or EN 13163 |
| Density | 15 — 35 kg/m³, **stated** |
| Thermal conductivity | Approximately 0.033 — 0.040 W/mK |
| Water absorption | Moderate; higher than XPS |
| Fire class | Combustible; FR grades available |
| Forms | Board, block, moulded shapes, panel core |

## Where it is appropriate

Dry above-ground thermal insulation · cold room and chiller panels where fire
strategy permits · void formers and lightweight fill in civil work ·
under-screed insulation in dry conditions · packaging and protection during
fit-out.

## Where it is not

**Ventilated facade cavities.** EPS in a cavity behind a combustible panel is
the assembly behind the most serious cladding fires. This is not a marginal
judgement.

**Wet or below-grade positions**, where its water absorption degrades
performance — XPS is the material there.

**Anywhere solvents are present.** Solvent-based adhesives and sealants
dissolve polystyrene, and this catches people out on site.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Density unspecified**, so the lightest grade arrives.
- **Used in facade cavities** on buildings where fire governs.
- **Assumed interchangeable with XPS** in wet or loaded positions.
- **Solvent adhesives** applied directly to the board.
- **Left exposed to sunlight**, which degrades the surface quickly.

## Standards referenced

EPS for thermal insulation under **IS 4671** and **EN 13163**. Reaction-to-fire
classification to **EN 13501-1**.
