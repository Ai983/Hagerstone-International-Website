## What it is

**Titanium zinc sheet** — zinc alloyed with small amounts of titanium and
copper — formed into standing seam, cassette or shingle cladding. It develops a
protective patina in service and is one of the longest-lived facade metals.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | EN 988 |
| Thickness | 0.7mm — 1.0mm typical |
| Finish | Mill, pre-weathered grey, or pigmented |
| System | Standing seam, cassette, interlocking shingle |
| Backing | **Ventilated and drained cavity, always** |
| Fire class | A1, non-combustible, to EN 13501-1 |

## Why it gets specified

**Very long service life** in normal atmospheres, with a self-healing patina
that protects the metal rather than degrading it.

**No coating to maintain.** Unlike painted metal there is no film to chalk,
fade or recoat, which removes a whole category of facade maintenance.

**Formability.** Zinc takes complex curves, seams and shingle patterns that
stiffer metals resist.

## The rear-face rule

This is the single thing that determines whether a zinc facade lasts:

- Continuous ventilation behind the panel, open at the bottom and top
- A separating layer where zinc meets timber, concrete or plaster
- No standing water anywhere against the rear face
- **No copper upstream.** Run-off from copper flashings or pipes destroys zinc
  downstream of it

## Where projects go wrong

- **Sealed cavity behind the panel.** The facade then corrodes from the back,
  invisibly, until it perforates.
- **Copper and zinc on the same elevation** with run-off from one onto the
  other.
- **Fixed rigidly.** Zinc has high thermal movement; clips must allow the panel
  to slide.
- **Handled with bare hands in hot weather.** Fingerprints can mark
  pre-weathered surfaces.
- **Working the metal below about 10°C** without warming, where zinc becomes
  brittle and cracks on folding.

## Standards referenced

Rolled zinc for building under **EN 988**. Reaction-to-fire classification to
**EN 13501-1**. Wind loading on cladding under **IS 875 (Part 3)**.
