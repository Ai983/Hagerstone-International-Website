## What it is

**Glass fibre reinforced polyester sheet**, profiled to match the surrounding
metal roof sheet. Translucent, rigid, cheap, and widely used for industrial
roof lights and wall lights across India.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Composition | Polyester resin with chopped glass fibre |
| Weight | Commonly 1,800 — 2,400 g/m² |
| Surface | Gel coat or surfacing film, UV protective |
| Light transmission | Stated as a percentage |
| Profile | Matched to the adjacent metal sheet |
| Fire class | Named EN 13501-1 class |

## The specification that decides service life

FRP is sold by weight per square metre, which sets stiffness and strength, but
**service life is set by the surface protection**. Once UV has eroded the resin
at the surface, the glass fibres stand proud, the sheet goes rough and opaque,
and it embrittles.

So the order should state the **surfacing system**, not just the gram weight.

## Where it belongs

Industrial and warehouse roof lights · wall lights in shed elevations ·
canopies and covered walkways where cost governs · temporary and short-life
structures · buildings where the roof sees little handling and impact risk is
low.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Weight specified, protection not.** The sheet then fails early and everyone
  blames FRP rather than the specification.
- **Fixings without oversized holes.** FRP moves with temperature and cracks
  around pinned screws.
- **Sheet used as a walking surface.** A genuine safety hazard.
- **End laps too short for the slope**, siphoning water back.
- **Mixed profiles**, so the FRP does not nest correctly with the metal sheet
  and the side lap never seals.

## Standards referenced

Wind loading on roof cladding under **IS 875 (Part 3)**. Skylight provisions
under **ECBC 2017**. Reaction-to-fire classification to **EN 13501-1**.
