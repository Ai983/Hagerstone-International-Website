## What it is

Two thin aluminium skins bonded to an **aluminium honeycomb core**, giving a
panel that is very stiff relative to its weight. The core is foil formed into
hexagonal cells; the skins carry the load and the core keeps them apart.

It is the flattest large-format metal panel available in normal facade
practice.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Skins | 0.5mm — 1.0mm aluminium to IS 737 |
| Core | Aluminium honeycomb, cell size by grade |
| Total thickness | 10mm — 25mm typical |
| Fire class | High classification; mineral throughout |
| Finish | PVDF coil coating or anodised |
| Edge | Bonded return or closed edge profile, always |

## Why it gets specified

**Flatness at size.** A 1.5m × 3m honeycomb panel holds a flat face where the
same size in 3mm solid sheet would need extensive rear stiffening and might
still show waviness.

**Weight.** Substantially lighter than solid aluminium of equivalent stiffness,
which reduces sub-frame and bracket loads on tall elevations.

**Fire position.** An all-aluminium build-up removes the polymer core question
from the fire submission entirely.

## Where it belongs

Entrance canopies and soffits · prestige lobby and atrium linings · large flat
spandrel and feature panels · column cladding on view · anywhere a designer has
specified a visually seamless plane at large panel size.

## What drives the cost

Skin thickness · core grade and panel thickness · finish class · panel size ·
edge treatment and the number of closures · curved or tapered geometry ·
bespoke fabrication, since honeycomb is rarely a stock item.

It sits **above solid aluminium** per square metre and well above ACP. The
specification is justified by flatness, not by price.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Open edges.** An unclosed cell edge takes water and dirt into the core.
  Every edge needs a bonded return or profile.
- **Panels at impact height.** A dented honeycomb panel is replaced, not
  repaired.
- **Fixing through the panel face** without a designed insert. Point loads
  crush the core locally.
- **Assuming stock availability.** These are made to order; the lead time is
  part of the programme, not a procurement detail.

## Standards referenced

Skin alloy under **IS 737**. Reaction-to-fire classification to
**EN 13501-1**. Structural performance verification to **ASTM E330**.
