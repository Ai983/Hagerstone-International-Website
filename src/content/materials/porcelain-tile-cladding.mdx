  - question: "Why choose porcelain over natural stone on a facade?"
    answer: "Consistency, weight and cost. Porcelain is manufactured, so colour and pattern repeat predictably across an elevation where natural stone varies block to block.   - question: "Does facade porcelain need back reinforcement?"
    answer: "For large formats on a ventilated facade, yes. A fibreglass mesh bonded to the rear holds the tile together if it cracks, so a failed panel does not fall in pieces from height. It is a safety requirement rather than a manufacturing nicety, and it should be specified explicitly along with the fixing system that suits it."

## What it is

**Large-format porcelain panels**, typically 3mm to 20mm thick, hung
mechanically on a ventilated facade. Manufactured stone and concrete
reproductions have made it a direct alternative to natural stone cladding.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | EN 14411, group BIa |
| Water absorption | Below 0.5%, effectively impervious |
| Thickness | 6mm — 20mm for facade use |
| Back reinforcement | Fibreglass mesh on large formats |
| Fixing | Visible clip, or rear undercut anchor on rails |
| Fire class | A1, non-combustible |

## Against natural stone

| | Porcelain | Natural stone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appearance consistency | Predictable and repeatable | Varies by block |
| Weight | Lighter | Heavier |
| Water absorption | Below 0.5% | Varies; some stones high |
| Staining and spalling | Very resistant | Depends on porosity |
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Perception | Manufactured | Genuine material |

## What the specification must cover

Panel size and thickness · back-mesh reinforcement requirement · fixing system
and whether anchors are undercut or clip · rail layout and wind pressure from
IS 875 Part 3 · joint width · edge and corner details, which are the visible
weak point of any large-format tile facade.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Adhesive fixing above low level.** The bond is uninspectable and the
  failure mode is a panel falling.
- **No back reinforcement** on large panels.
- **Rail spacing set by convenience** rather than wind calculation.
- **Interior-grade tile specified for a facade**; the two are not the same
  product.
- **Corner details left to site.** Mitred porcelain corners are fragile and
  need designed protection.

## Standards referenced

Ceramic tile classification under **EN 14411**. Reaction-to-fire classification
to **EN 13501-1**. Wind loading under **IS 875 (Part 3)**.
