## What the grade decision turns on

Most office joinery is built from plywood: workstation carcasses, storage
walls, reception desks, wall panelling and pantry units. The grade printed on
the sheet is mainly about **the glue bond and how it behaves when wet**, not
about strength or appearance. That distinction is where money is lost.

A common misconception is that "BWP" or "marine" means a better board in every
way. It means a more water-resistant bond. A BWP board with a gappy core still
sags, strips screws and telegraphs through laminate. Equally, specifying marine
plywood across a dry office floor buys protection nobody needs. The cost of
getting it wrong shows up a year after handover: delaminating vanity units,
swollen pantry shutters, and storage doors that no longer close, each one a
strip-out and remake in an occupied office.

## Grades at a glance

| Grade | Standard | Bond type | Where it suits | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MR (moisture resistant) | IS 303 | Usually urea-formaldehyde | Dry interiors: carcasses, wardrobes, panelling away from water | Delaminates if wetted; often the highest formaldehyde release |
| BWR (boiling water resistant) | IS 303 | Phenolic | Humid areas, AC plant rooms, joinery near glazing | Grade name alone says nothing about the core |
| BWP (boiling water proof) | IS 303 | Phenolic, tougher test | Pantries, under-sink units, washroom vanities | Cut edges still need sealing |
| Marine | IS 710 | Boiling water proof, plus species and preservative controls | Joinery repeatedly wetted, high humidity zones | Costs more; rarely needed on dry floors |
| Fire retardant | IS 5509 | Treated plywood | Where the fire strategy asks for a treated lining | Acceptance is the fire consultant's decision |

The bond tests themselves, including glue adhesion after boiling and moisture
content, are set out in **IS 1734**. The 2024 revision of **IS 303** brought a
BWP grade into the general-purpose standard alongside MR and BWR; **IS 710**
remains the standard for marine plywood. In European specifications the same
idea appears as use classes under **EN 636**: dry, humid and exterior.

## Core species and construction

The grade covers the glue; the **core** decides how the board works:

- **Hardwood cores** of denser species hold screws and hinges better and
  resist sagging over shelf spans
- **Softer or mixed cores** are lighter and cheaper but strip screws faster,
  which shows first at hinge and drawer-runner positions
- **Core gaps and overlaps** inside the board create hollow spots that read
  through thin laminate as dents and ripples
- **Face veneer quality** matters only where the board is veneered or polished;
  under a laminate, a flat, void-free core matters more

Thickness is typically chosen by span and load: thinner boards for backs and
drawer bottoms, thicker for carcasses, shelves and desk tops. The joinery
designer and the manufacturer's data set the actual thickness for each part.

## What grade goes where in an office

| Location | Usual approach |
| --- | --- |
| Workstation and storage carcasses on dry floors | MR or BWR with a dense, gap-free core |
| Wall panelling on internal walls | MR or BWR, balanced laminate or veneer both faces |
| Panelling on external walls or near glazing | BWR or BWP, with the wall checked for damp first |
| Pantry units and anything near a sink | BWP or marine, edges sealed; consider a waterproof board under the sink |
| Washroom vanities and duct doors | BWP or marine, or a fully waterproof board |
| Corridor and escape route linings | As the fire strategy directs, which may rule plywood out |

For the finish on top of these boards, see
[laminate and veneer for joinery](/materials/laminate-and-veneer). Where water
is permanent, a [WPC or PVC foam board](/materials/wpc-board) usually lasts
longer than any plywood grade. Where a painted or routed finish is wanted, an
[MDF or HDHMR board](/materials/mdf-board) is often the better substrate.

## Formaldehyde emission classes

Plywood is bonded with formaldehyde-based resins, and some free formaldehyde is
released into the room, highest when the board is new. It is measured by a
chamber method such as **EN 717-1**, and boards are then sold against an
emission class (E1 and lower-emission classes are the terms most buyers meet).
The class is a property of the tested product, not of the grade. For an office
that will be occupied soon after fit-out, ask for the emission test report and
plan ventilation before move-in.

## How to check plywood on site

- **Print on the board**: grade, standard number, thickness and the BIS licence
  details, checked against the approved sample and the invoice
- **Thickness**: measured with a vernier at several points; undersize boards are
  common
- **Edges**: cut a sample and look for core gaps, overlaps and soft layers
- **Tap test**: hollow sound over the face suggests voids
- **Moisture**: boards stored wet or on a damp slab will move after fixing

Lock the approved sample before bulk ordering; the process is set out in
[material samples and approvals before ordering](/blog/material-samples-and-approvals-before-ordering).

## Common mistakes

- **Grade chosen, core ignored.** The BOQ says only "BWP plywood" and the
  cheapest core arrives.
- **MR plywood under the pantry sink.** It delaminates within a couple of
  monsoons.
- **Unsealed cut edges.** Even BWP swells through a raw edge left behind a
  basin.
- **Laminate on one face only.** The panel bows, whatever the grade.
- **Boards stored on a damp slab** for weeks before fabrication.
- **No sample retained**, so there is nothing to compare deliveries against.

## What to ask your contractor or joinery supplier

- Which grade and standard is each joinery item made from?
- What core species and construction is the board?
- Is there an emission class and a test report for this product?
- How are cut edges sealed in wet areas?
- Will a sample sheet be kept on site for comparing deliveries?

## Standards referenced

General-purpose plywood under **IS 303**, marine plywood under **IS 710**, test
methods under **IS 1734**, and fire retardant plywood under **IS 5509**. Use
classes under **EN 636** and formaldehyde release by the chamber method under
**EN 717-1**. Fire performance of linings in a building is decided under NBC 2016
Part 4. The grade, thickness and treatment for each joinery item must be
confirmed by the project's interior designer, joinery engineer and, for
linings, the fire consultant.
