## What it is

**Rigid closed-cell extruded polystyrene board**, manufactured as a continuous
extrusion so the cell structure is uniform and almost impermeable to water.

That moisture resistance is the reason it exists alongside cheaper EPS.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | EN 13164 |
| Thermal conductivity | Approximately 0.029 — 0.035 W/mK |
| Compressive strength | 200 — 700 kPa by grade |
| Water absorption | Very low; closed cell |
| Edge | Square, shiplap or tongue and groove |
| Fire class | Combustible; class stated |

## Where it belongs

| Application | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Inverted / protected-membrane roofs | Survives above the waterproofing |
| Below-grade walls and rafts | Resists ground moisture |
| Under floor slabs | Takes load and stays dry |
| Perimeter insulation | Handles backfill contact |
| Cold store floors | Combines strength and moisture resistance |

## Where it does not belong

Ventilated facade cavities on buildings of any height, escape routes, and
anywhere fire performance governs. It is combustible, and in a cavity that is
the wrong material regardless of its thermal numbers.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Compressive grade unspecified**, then surface depressions appear under
  paving or traffic.
- **Used in facade cavities** where non-combustible insulation is required.
- **Left exposed to sunlight** on site; UV degrades the surface.
- **Solvent-based adhesives** used with it, which dissolve polystyrene.
- **Board joints left open** below grade, creating thermal bypass paths through
  the insulation layer.

## Standards referenced

XPS product requirements under **EN 13164**. Opaque assembly U-value under
**ECBC 2017**. Reaction-to-fire classification to **EN 13501-1**.
