## What the decision turns on

Paint is the largest wet finish in most office fit-outs, and it goes on in
the last weeks, often days before the move-in date. That timing is why VOCs
matter commercially. **VOCs** (volatile organic compounds) are carbon-based
chemicals that evaporate from paints, adhesives and sealants into the air as
they dry and for a period afterwards. A freshly painted floor that smells on
day one generates complaints, help-desk tickets and sometimes a delayed
move-in.

The common misconception is that a "low-VOC" label settles the matter. It does
not, for three reasons: the label means nothing without a test method and a
limit; VOC **content** in the can is not the same as VOC **emission** into the
room; and the tint, primer and the ventilation before occupation can matter
as much as the base paint.

## How low-VOC is defined

| Term | What it measures | How it is tested | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VOC content | VOC in the liquid paint, usually as mass per volume | Gas chromatography, e.g. **ISO 11890-2** | Whether the product meets a content limit |
| VOC emission | VOC released into room air over time from a painted sample | Emission test chamber, e.g. **ISO 16000-9** or **EN 16516** | How the product behaves on the wall after application |
| Product standard limit | Maximum VOC allowed for that paint type | **IS 15489** for plastic emulsion paint | Whether the paint conforms to the Indian Standard |
| Rating system threshold | Content or emission limits for credits | Set by the green building rating system | Whether the product counts for certification |

**IS 15489** is the Indian Standard for plastic emulsion paint used on
interior and exterior building surfaces. Its revision introduced limits on VOC
content, including a provision for colourant added at the point of sale, and
groups products into matt, eggshell or satin, semi-gloss and glossy finishes.
**ISO 11890-2** is the gas-chromatographic method used to measure VOC content.
Emission testing under **ISO 16000-9** or **EN 16516** puts a painted sample in
a controlled chamber and measures what it releases into the air over days.

When a supplier says "low-VOC", ask for three things: the figure, the test
method, and the limit it is compared against.

## Why it matters at handover and occupancy

VOC release is highest while paint dries and declines over the following days
and weeks. What people notice is the combination of the paint, the adhesives
under flooring, new furniture boards and sealants, all released into a space
whose ventilation may still be in commissioning. This page is about the paint;
the wider picture of sustainable fit-out choices is discussed in
[sustainable and green office interiors](/blog/sustainable-green-office-interiors).

General indoor air quality guidance treats reducing pollutant sources and
diluting them with outdoor air as the two main levers. Outdoor air rates for
offices are set by the HVAC designer under **ASHRAE 62.1**; how that works in
practice is explained in
[office CO2 and fresh air under ASHRAE 62.1](/blog/office-co2-and-fresh-air-ashrae-62-1).

## Flush-out before occupation

- **Sequence the trades**: paint and other wet finishes before carpets, fabric
  panels and upholstered furniture, which can absorb VOCs and release them
  later
- **Run the HVAC on outdoor air** after wet trades and before move-in, for a
  period the HVAC engineer sets from the system capacity and any rating-system
  requirement
- **Protect the ductwork** during painting and sanding, and replace
  construction-phase filters before the flush-out
- **Programme it**: a flush-out squeezed out of the last week is the most
  common reason it does not happen

Record the flush-out in the handover file alongside commissioning results; see
[turnkey office fit-out](/services/interior-fit-out/turnkey-office-fit-out) for
how finishing, commissioning and handover are sequenced.

## Finishes: durability vs washability

| Finish | Strengths | Limits | Typical office use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Matt | Hides wall defects, low glare | Marks easily; less washable | Ceilings, large open walls |
| Eggshell / satin | Washable, durable | Shows wall defects more | Corridors, meeting rooms, pantries |
| Semi-gloss | Highly washable | Shows every defect | Doors, frames, high-wear dados |
| Gloss | Hardest wearing, easiest to clean | Glare and defects | Trim, service areas |

Where walls take constant impact rather than just marks, a wallcovering may
outlast any paint; compare
[commercial vinyl wallcovering](/materials/vinyl-wallcovering).

## Common mistakes

- **"Low-VOC" accepted with no figure or method.** The claim cannot be checked
  at handover.
- **Deep tints ordered without checking tinted VOC**, so the feature wall is
  the highest-VOC surface on the floor.
- **Solvent-based primers or enamels under low-VOC topcoats**, undoing the
  point.
- **Carpets and furniture installed before painting finishes**, absorbing what
  the paint releases.
- **No flush-out**, because the HVAC was still being commissioned on move-in
  weekend.
- **Matt paint in corridors**, scuffed within weeks and repainted.

## What to ask your contractor or paint supplier

- What is the VOC content of the tinted product, by which method, against
  which limit?
- Is there an emission test report for this product?
- Do the primer, putty and enamel meet the same requirement?
- Which finish class is proposed for each area?
- When, and for how long, will the flush-out run, and who signs it off?

## Standards referenced

Plastic emulsion paint under **IS 15489**. VOC content by the
gas-chromatographic method of **ISO 11890-2**. Emissions into indoor air by
chamber testing under **ISO 16000-9** and **EN 16516**. Office ventilation under
**ASHRAE 62.1**. Product selection, VOC limits for a rating system and the
flush-out procedure for a particular building must be confirmed by the
project's designer, HVAC engineer and, where one applies, the rating-system
consultant.
