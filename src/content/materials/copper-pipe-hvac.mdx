faqs:
  - question: "Can plumbing copper be used for refrigerant lines?"
    answer: "No. Refrigeration work requires ACR grade tube to ASTM B280 or equivalent, which is cleaned, dehydrated and sealed at both ends at the factory. Ordinary plumbing tube carries internal oxides, drawing oils and moisture that contaminate the refrigerant circuit, damage the compressor and block expansion devices. The two look identical on site, which is exactly why substitution happens."
  - question: "Why must brazing be done under a nitrogen purge?"
    answer: "Because heating copper in air forms a black cupric oxide scale on the inside of the tube. That scale flakes off in the refrigerant stream and circulates through the system, scoring the compressor and clogging filters and expansion valves. Flowing dry nitrogen through the pipe during brazing displaces the oxygen so no scale forms. Skipping it is invisible at the joint and destroys compressors months later."
  - question: "What insulation do refrigerant lines need?"
    answer: "Closed-cell elastomeric insulation, sized for the line temperature and the ambient humidity, with every joint glued rather than taped. On suction lines running well below dew point in Indian humidity, insufficient thickness causes condensation to form on the pipe, which then drips into ceilings and stains them. Thickness should be calculated for local conditions, not taken as a default figure."

## What it is

**Seamless copper tube** used for refrigerant lines, chilled water and
condensate. In refrigeration it must be ACR grade — cleaned, dried and capped
at manufacture.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Grade | ACR to ASTM B280, or IS 2501 for general use |
| Temper | Hard drawn straight lengths, or soft annealed coils |
| Wall thickness | By diameter and system pressure |
| Supply condition | Nitrogen charged and capped, for ACR |
| Jointing | Brazed under nitrogen purge |
| Insulation | Closed-cell elastomeric, thickness calculated |

## Hard drawn against soft annealed

| | Hard drawn | Soft annealed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form | Straight lengths | Coils |
| Bending | Fittings required | Bends by tool |
| Appearance | Neat, rigid runs | More flexible routing |
| Support spacing | Wider | Closer |
| Use | Main risers and long runs | Final connections, short runs |

## Non-negotiables on refrigerant work

1. **ACR grade tube only**, kept capped until the moment of use
2. **Nitrogen purge during brazing**, every joint, without exception
3. **Pressure test and deep vacuum** before charging
4. **Insulation continuous**, glued at joints, thickness calculated for local
   dew point
5. **Pipe supported** without crushing the insulation at hangers

## Where projects go wrong

- **Plumbing copper used for refrigerant.** Contamination, then compressor
  failure.
- **Brazing without nitrogen**, producing internal scale.
- **Tube left uncapped** on site, taking in humid air and dust.
- **Insulation taped rather than glued**, opening at the joints and sweating.
- **Under-thickness insulation**, causing condensation drips into ceilings.

## Standards referenced

Copper tube for general engineering under **IS 2501**. ACR tube under
**ASTM B280**. HVAC piping insulation requirements under **ECBC 2017**.
