  - question: "Why do VRF systems have maximum pipe lengths?"
    answer: "Because refrigerant carries compressor oil around the circuit, and beyond a certain total length, height difference or branch count, the gas velocity is too low to drag oil back to the compressor. Oil then accumulates in the far parts of the system, the compressor runs short of lubrication, and it fails. The limits published for each system are not conservative guidance — they are the boundary of where oil return works."
  - question: "What are branch joints and why must they be the manufacturer's?"
    answer: "Branch joints, often called refnets, split the refrigerant flow to serve multiple indoor units. Their internal geometry is designed so that both liquid and gas distribute correctly and oil keeps moving. A fabricated tee made on site has none of that geometry, so distribution becomes uneven and units underperform or trip. The joints must be the manufacturer's parts, installed in the stated orientation."

## What it is

The refrigerant pipework connecting a VRF outdoor unit to multiple indoor
units, with branch joints distributing flow. It is a **designed system**, not
just pipe run to suit the building.

## What the design must respect

| Limit | Why it exists |
| --- | --- |
| Total pipe length | Pressure drop and oil return |
| Longest single run | Capacity loss at the far unit |
| Height difference, outdoor to indoor | Oil return against gravity |
| Height difference between indoor units | Distribution balance |
| Length after first branch | Distribution balance |
| Number of branches | System capability |

Every manufacturer publishes these. Exceeding any of them voids the warranty
and, more practically, produces a system that underperforms or fails early.

## Installation essentials

- **ACR grade copper** to ASTM B280, capped until use
- **Manufacturer's branch joints** only, in the correct orientation
- **Nitrogen purge** during every brazed joint
- **Pressure test**, then deep vacuum to the stated micron level
- **Charge calculated** from as-built lengths and diameters
- **Insulation on both liquid and gas lines**, continuous and glued
- **Traps and risers** detailed per the manual where long vertical runs occur

## Where projects go wrong

- **Site-fabricated tees** instead of branch joints.
- **Pipe routes extended** during coordination until a limit is quietly
  exceeded.
- **Vacuum cut short** because the programme is tight, leaving moisture in the
  circuit which forms acid and destroys the compressor.
- **Charge estimated** rather than calculated from as-built pipe runs.
- **Liquid line left uninsulated**, reducing capacity and causing condensation.

ACR copper tube under **ASTM B280**. Refrigerating system safety under
