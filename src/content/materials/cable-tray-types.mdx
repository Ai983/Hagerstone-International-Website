  - question: "Why does tray fill matter?"
    answer: "Because bunched cables cannot shed heat. Cables sized from a catalogue rating assume a particular installation condition, and packing a tray full changes that condition significantly — the grouping derating factor under IS 3961 can reduce capacity substantially. An overfilled tray turns correctly sized cable into undersized cable, and the effect is invisible until something runs hot for years."

## What it is

The **support system that carries cables** through ceiling voids, shafts and
plant rooms. Chosen by load, cable type, environment and access needs.

| Type | Best for | Load |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ladder** | Heavy power cables, long spans | High |
| **Perforated** | Mixed power and control | Medium |
| **Wire mesh basket** | Data, light cabling, tight voids | Light |
| **Trunking** | Protected runs, surface installations | Light to medium |

## Finish by environment

| Finish | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-galvanised | Dry indoor areas |
| Hot dip galvanised | Humid, semi-exposed, plant rooms |
| Stainless 316 | Coastal, chemical, food and pharma |
| Powder coated | Exposed architectural runs |

## What the specification must cover

Tray type and size · finish and coating thickness · **fill ratio limit** ·
support spacing and the load at that spacing · earthing continuity method ·
separation between power and data · fire-rated support where cables serve life
safety systems — a tray that fails in a fire drops a fire-survival cable and
defeats its purpose.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Trays overfilled**, derating the cables invisibly.
- **Earth continuity broken** at painted bolted joints.
- **Power and data in the same tray** with no separation, causing interference.
- **Support spacing too wide**, so loaded trays sag.
- **Plastic supports on fire-survival cable routes**, which melt and drop the
  cable early in a fire.
- **Cut edges left unprotected** after site modification, corroding and cutting
  cable sheaths.

## Standards referenced

Wiring installation practice under **IS 732**. Cable tray and ladder systems
under **IEC 61537**. Cable derating for grouping under **IS 3961**.
