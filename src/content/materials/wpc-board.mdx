## What it is

Two related board types used where water rules out plywood:

- **WPC** — wood-plastic composite, wood flour in a polymer matrix
- **PVC foam board** — rigid closed-cell PVC with no wood content

Both are fully waterproof through their thickness.

## Specification at a glance

| Property | Typical specification |
| --- | --- |
| Density | 550 — 750 kg/m³; **state it** |
| Thickness | 6mm — 25mm |
| Water absorption | Effectively nil |
| Screw holding | Lower than plywood; grade dependent |
| Finish | Laminate faced, foil wrapped, or painted |
| Fire class | Combustible; class stated |

## Where it belongs

Washroom vanity carcasses and cubicle framing · pantry base units, especially
under sinks · service duct access doors · utility and housekeeping storage ·
skirting and architraves in wet zones · locations where termite risk rules out
timber.

## Where plywood remains better

Long shelf spans under load · structural joinery carcasses · anywhere screw
pull-out strength matters · dry office joinery generally, where plywood is
stronger, stiffer and cheaper for the same performance.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Density unspecified**, so a low grade arrives and shelves sag.
- **Detailed like plywood**, with the same spans and standard screws.
- **Long runs fixed rigidly**, then buckling with temperature.
- **Fire class ignored** where surface spread of flame is regulated.
- **Used everywhere on cost grounds**, producing weak joinery in dry areas that
  plywood would have served better.

## Standards referenced

Plywood comparison baseline under **IS 303**. Composite board testing practice
per **ASTM D6662**. Reaction-to-fire classification to **EN 13501-1**.
