## The mix matters more than the count

| Room type | Typical demand | Typical provision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone booth, 1 person | Constant | Rarely enough |
| Small room, 2–4 | Highest of all | Under-provided |
| Medium room, 6–8 | Moderate | Usually adequate |
| Large room, 10–16 | Occasional | Over-provided |
| Board room | Infrequent | Always provided |

Booking data from most offices shows the same shape. Large rooms are booked for
small meetings because nothing smaller is free, which makes the shortage worse
than the numbers suggest.

If booking data exists, use it. If not, err toward more small rooms.

## Under-providing small rooms damages the open plan

This is the argument worth making to whoever controls the budget.

Without enough booths and small rooms, calls and quick discussions happen at
desks. That is the single largest source of open-plan noise complaints — so the
cost of under-providing small enclosed spaces is not borne by the people who
wanted a room, it is borne by everyone on the floor.

## What makes a room actually usable

**Fresh air sized for the room's occupancy.** A small room with six people has a
far higher occupant density than the open plan, and ventilating it at the same
rate produces the stuffiness that empties meetings after twenty minutes.
Ventilation rates are commonly referenced to **ASHRAE 62.1**.

**Acoustic separation that accounts for flanking.** Partition to slab, junctions
sealed, services crossings treated, outlets offset. A rating under
**ISO 717-1** describes a wall without those paths.

**Lighting suited to both faces and screens.** Interior illumination practice is
set out in **IS 3646**; a room lit only for the table is unflattering on video
calls and glares on the screen.

**AV decided before construction.** Screen size sets the viewing distance, which
sets the table position, which sets the room dimensions. Working the other way
produces rooms where people cannot read the screen from the far end.

## Practical planning points

- **Do not line the facade with meeting rooms.** They take daylight from the
  entire floor behind them; glaze them if they must sit there.
- **Provide visibility into rooms**, through glazing or a vision panel, so people
  can see whether a room is free without opening the door.
- **Think about the door swing** — inward into a room with a table costs usable
  space.
- **Booths need ventilation too.** A sealed phone booth without air supply
  becomes unusable within minutes.
- **Consider furniture depth**, not just the table. Circulation around a seated
  person is what sets the real room size.

## Compliance

Subdividing a floor changes escape arrangements, travel distances, occupancy
assessment, and detection and sprinkler layouts. Those are determined under
**NBC 2016, Part 4** and the applicable local rules by the fire consultant and
the authority having jurisdiction.

Check while the layout is a drawing.

## Standards referenced

Fire and life safety provisions in **NBC 2016, Part 4**; ventilation rates
commonly referenced to **ASHRAE 62.1**; sound insulation rating in
**ISO 717-1**; interior illumination practice in **IS 3646**. Occupancy
assessment, escape arrangements, ventilation rates and acoustic targets for a
specific project must be established by the project's consultants.
