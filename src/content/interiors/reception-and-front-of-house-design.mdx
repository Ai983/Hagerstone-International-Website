## Four problems in one room

| Problem | What it needs |
| --- | --- |
| Arrival experience | A legible sequence; no hesitation on entry |
| Reception's actual work | Storage, screens, deliveries, passes, phone |
| Waiting at peak | Capacity for batches, not averages |
| Durability | Finishes that survive the building's heaviest traffic |

Most receptions resolve the first well and one or more of the others badly.

## The arrival sequence

A visitor entering should know, without asking, where to go. That means the
reception position is visible from the entrance, or wayfinding makes it obvious
immediately.

Hesitation is the failure state. A visitor pausing to work out where to stand is
having a worse experience than the materials are improving.

Sequence questions worth settling:

- Where does the visitor's eye land first on entering?
- Is the desk visible, or does something obstruct it?
- Where do they wait, and can they see they are being attended to?
- How do they get from waiting to the meeting room, and who takes them?
- Where do deliveries and couriers go, so they do not cross the visitor route?

## The desk does more than greeting

A reception desk usually handles deliveries and parcels, pass issue and
collection, keys, the phone, and often access control and CCTV screens.

Designed as a pure object, all of that ends up on the counter or on the floor
behind it — visible, untidy, and exactly the impression the design was meant to
avoid.

Specify the storage, the screen positions, the cable management and the parcel
space as part of the design, not as an afterthought.

## Acoustics in a hard room

Reception palettes tend toward stone, glass and exposed ceilings, all reflective.
The result is a space where conversation and footfall build up.

Absorption can be introduced without compromising the palette — an absorbent
ceiling zone, a treated wall panel behind a feature finish, or soft seating
placed deliberately. Absorption performance comes from data measured under
**ISO 354**, and the quantity is calculable at design stage.

Lighting matters alongside it: interior illumination practice is set out in
**IS 3646**, and a reception needs both the general level and the feature
lighting that makes materials read properly.

## Durability at the points that take wear

- **Entrance matting**, sized properly — the single most effective protection
  for every floor finish beyond it
- **Flooring** in the first few metres, which takes grit and water
- **Desk edges and corners**, which take bag and trolley knocks
- **Wall corners** on routes used for deliveries
- **Glass manifestation**, designed rather than added later as stickers

## Escape and occupancy

Reception sits on the main entrance and exit route. Furniture layout, feature
elements and queuing positions must not obstruct doors or routes, and occupancy
considerations apply.

These are determined under **NBC 2016, Part 4** by the fire consultant and the
authority having jurisdiction — and, as with any layout decision, checking at
drawing stage is far cheaper than after installation.

## Standards referenced

Fire and life safety provisions, including exits and occupancy, in
**NBC 2016, Part 4**; interior illumination practice in **IS 3646**; sound
absorption measurement in **ISO 354**. Escape arrangements and occupancy
assessment for a specific space must be established by the project's fire
consultant and the authority having jurisdiction.
