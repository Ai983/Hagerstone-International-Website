## What we deliver

**Lighting design and installation for offices** — lux levels, layouts, fitting
selection, controls and emergency lighting, integrated with the ceiling design
rather than laid over it.

Most office lighting is not designed. Fittings get spaced evenly on a grid until
the ceiling looks balanced, and the result is a floor that meets an average
number while being uncomfortable to work under.

## Scope of works

| Stage | What we deliver |

## Typical design targets

| Zone | Working-plane lux | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open plan desks | 300-500 | Uniformity matters as much as the average |
| Meeting rooms | 300-500 | Dimmable for presentation |
| Reception | 200-300 | Feature and accent lighting |
| Circulation | 150-200 | Lower, with emergency coverage |
| Cafeteria | 200-300 | Warmer colour temperature |
| Detailed / drawing work | 500-750 | Task lighting supplements general |

## Where projects go wrong

- **Designing to average lux and ignoring uniformity.** Bright pools with dark
  gaps read as a badly lit office regardless of the average.
- **No glare control.** Screen reflections and direct glare make an otherwise
  adequate floor unpleasant to work in.
- **Lighting drawn after the ceiling.** Fittings then compete with diffusers,
  sprinklers and detectors for the same tile.
- **Mixed colour temperature.** Fittings from different batches or suppliers on
  one floor are immediately visible.
- **Emergency lighting left to the end.** Escape route coverage is a compliance
  requirement and needs to be in the layout, not added afterwards.
