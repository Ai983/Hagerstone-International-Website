## What we deliver

**HVAC, electrical, plumbing, firefighting, low voltage and networking**
coordinated into the fit-out — designed against the actual layout rather than
inherited from the base build and hoped to still work.

This is the package that decides whether a finished office is comfortable. It is
also where the majority of fit-out delays and post-handover complaints originate,
because it sits at the interface between trades that are usually contracted
separately.

## What changes when a floor is subdivided

| Base build assumes | Your layout does | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open plan | Cabins and meeting rooms | Sprinkler heads relocated per enclosure |
| Even cooling load | Rooms with different occupancy | HVAC zoning and rebalancing |
| Free return air path | Walls to slab | Transfer grilles or ducted return |
| Uniform lighting grid | Task-specific zones | Circuits and controls redesigned |
| Generic small power | Desk and equipment positions | Floor boxes and containment reset |
| Open detection coverage | Enclosed rooms | Detectors added per compartment |

## Where projects go wrong

- **HVAC never rebalanced after subdivision.** The single most common cause of
  comfort complaints in a new office.
- **Return air path blocked by partitions to slab.** Supply is fine, return is
  not, and the room feels stuffy despite cold air arriving.
- **Sprinkler coverage not reassessed.** Every enclosed room needs coverage in
  its own right under NBC 2016 Part 4.
- **Detection coverage missed in new rooms.** Compartments created by the
  fit-out need their own detection.
- **Containment undersized.** Data cabling grows; a tray sized exactly for
  day-one count means the next change is a rebuild.
