## What we deliver

**Premium corporate, hotel and hospitality interiors** — where the finish
standard, the materials and the guest-facing detail change how the project has to
be run, not just what it costs.

Luxury work is often described as the same fit-out with better materials. It is
not. It is a different programme shape: procurement leads, tolerances tighten,
mock-ups matter, and the snagging standard that would pass on a commercial floor
does not pass here.

## What changes on a premium project

| | Standard fit-out | Luxury / hospitality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finish tolerance | Millimetres | Sub-millimetre at reveals and joints |
| Materials | Locally available, short lead | Often imported, long lead |
| Programme driver | Site sequence | Procurement and lead times |
| Mock-ups | Rarely needed | Standard for key finishes |
| Snagging standard | Functional | Visual, at close inspection distance |
| Lighting | Task and general | Layered, with accent and feature |
| Back of house | Utilitarian | Designed around operations |

## Where projects go wrong

- **Procurement started late.** On a premium project, lead times set the
  handover date. Nothing on site recovers a slab ordered eight weeks late.
- **No mock-up on key finishes.** Stone, veneer and specialist plaster look
  different at scale than on a sample, and by then they are ordered.
- **Batch variation ignored.** Natural materials vary between batches; one
  elevation should come from one batch, which has to be planned at order stage.
- **Back of house treated as leftover space.** In hospitality it determines
  whether the property can actually be operated.
- **Commercial-grade snagging.** The standard that passes on an office floor
  will not pass in a hotel lobby.
