## What we deliver

**Complete office fit-out under one contract** — from an empty shell or a
landlord's Cat A floor through to a space your team can move into.

The reason clients consolidate this into one contract is not convenience. It is
that the expensive failures on a fit-out happen at the *interfaces* — between the
ceiling contractor and the sprinkler contractor, between the furniture layout and
the small power design, between the partition line and the HVAC zoning. When
those are one party's responsibility, they get resolved on a drawing instead of
on site.

## What drives the cost

Carpet area — never chargeable area · specification grade · cabin and meeting
room density, since partitions and their MEP knock-ons are expensive · furniture
specification, often the single largest line · whether the floor is Cat A or bare
shell · occupied versus empty · programme compression · finishes and branding.

## Where projects go wrong

- **Budgeting against chargeable area.** On a 30% loading factor a fit-out looks
  30% more expensive per square foot than it is. Fit-out is priced on carpet.
- **Assuming a Cat A floor is nearly finished.** It is deliberately generic, and
  Cat B undoes part of it — ceilings cut, sprinklers moved, HVAC rebalanced.
- **Freezing the layout after ceilings close.** Adding cabins changes escape
  routes, travel distance and occupancy under NBC 2016 Part 4. Cheap on a
  drawing, expensive after.
- **Ordering furniture late.** Long-lead joinery and workstations govern the
  handover date more often than site work does.
- **No landlord NOC in the programme.** Fit-out cannot legitimately start
  without it, and the approval sits outside your control.
