    answer: "Grid wins wherever the plenum needs to stay accessible, which is most open-plan office area — lifting a tile to reach a VAV box or a cable tray is far cheaper than cutting and re-skimming plasterboard. ## What we deliver

**Grid and gypsum ceilings, drywall, demountable and glass partitions** —
installed as part of the fit-out and coordinated with everything that runs above
and through them.

Ceilings and partitions look like the simplest package on a fit-out. They are
actually the package that touches every other trade: sprinklers, diffusers,
lighting, cable tray, smoke detection and speakers all land in or pass through
them, and every cabin wall interrupts all of it.

## Ceiling types

| Type | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mineral fibre grid | General open plan | Accessible; standard appearance |
| Metal grid | Corridors, wet areas | Durable; higher cost |
| Gypsum | Reception, curves, feature zones | Seamless; access needs traps |
| Acoustic baffles / rafts | Open plan noise control | Effective; requires coordination |
| Exposed services | Informal and creative offices | No ceiling cost; MEP must look intentional |

## Where projects go wrong

- **Reflected ceiling plan not coordinated before boarding.** Then diffusers,
  sprinklers and lights fight for the same tile and the ceiling looks accidental.
- **Meeting room partitions stopped at the ceiling.** Sound travels over the
  wall through the shared plenum. The room is visually private and acoustically
  not.
- **Compartmentation broken by the fit-out.** Partitions crossing a fire
  compartment line must maintain the rating, including at penetrations.
- **Access ignored.** A gypsum ceiling over a floor full of VAV boxes and valves
  means cutting it open the first time anything needs service.
- **Grid module set out from the wrong edge.** Cut tiles at the visible end
  rather than the concealed one — a small decision that reads as sloppy for the
  life of the fit-out.
