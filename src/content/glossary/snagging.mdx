## Why it matters on a project

Snagging is what the client remembers. Commissioning is more consequential, but
it is invisible; a chipped door frame in reception is not.

A fit-out that was technically excellent can still feel like a poor job at
handover if the finishing list is long, and a project with a modest budget can
feel meticulous if it is closed out well.

## Three things that get confused

| | What it is | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Snag** | Visible incomplete or poor-quality work | Before handover |
| **Defect** | A fault in the works that appears later | After handover |
| **Variation** | A change from what was specified | Any time — and it is chargeable |

The third one is where snag lists go wrong. A list containing genuine snags
mixed with client changes invites the contractor to dispute the whole thing.

## Snag progressively, not at the end

The single most useful practice here: inspect each area as it completes.

Done at the end, a snagging exercise produces hundreds of items at the exact
moment the programme has no slack, and some of them are in work that later
trades have already covered. Done progressively, most items are closed while the
relevant trade is still on the floor, and the final list is short.

## Running a list that closes

- **One list, one owner.** Parallel lists from different people never converge.
- **Location, description, photograph, date** on every item.
- **Separate variations out before issuing.** Price them separately, honestly.
- **Close items with a date and a note**, and re-inspect rather than accepting a
  verbal confirmation.
- **Agree the standard beforehand.** "Poor finish" is arguable; the
  specification is not. Where a standard of finish matters — a feature wall, a
  reception desk, visible joinery — say so before it is built.

## Common mistakes

- **One big inspection at the end.** Guarantees a list too long to close.
- **Mixing in changes of mind.** Undermines the credibility of everything else
  on the list.
- **Letting occupants snag unsupervised.** Useful input, but it needs filtering
  against what was specified.
- **No photographs.** Items become unarguable only when they are documented.
- **Treating snagging as a substitute for commissioning.** A snag-free floor can
  still be badly balanced, badly controlled and uncomfortable.
- **Occupying before the list is closed.** Access gets harder, damage gets mixed
  in with snags, and attribution becomes impossible.

## Standards referenced

process; the required standard of finish is set by the project's specification
