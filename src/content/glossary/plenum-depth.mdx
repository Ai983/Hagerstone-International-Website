## Why it matters on a fit-out project

Plenum depth is the constraint that quietly decides a large number of design
questions, and it is almost never discussed until it is a problem.

It determines the ceiling height. It determines whether the ductwork can run
where the drawing says. It determines whether a raised floor is possible. And
because slab-to-slab height was fixed when the building was built, none of it is
negotiable at fit-out stage.

## What competes for the space

| Element | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Structural downstands | Beams reduce the usable depth locally, often at the worst point |
| Main ductwork | The largest single consumer; depth drives everything else |
| Sprinkler pipework | Needs its own zone and falls, plus head positions |
| Lighting | Recessed fittings need depth above the ceiling |
| Cable containment | Trays and baskets, with room to pull cable |
| Ceiling suspension | Hangers, grid and the tile or board itself |
| Access allowance | Space to reach anything that will need maintenance |

## The number that matters is the worst point

Coordination fails when the plenum is assessed at a typical location rather than
at the tightest one — usually where a main duct crosses a downstand beam near a
core.

That intersection, not the average, is what sets the achievable ceiling height
across the floor, because a ceiling that steps around obstructions reads as a
series of bulkheads.

The reliable way to establish it is a **coordinated section through the worst
crossing**, drawn before the ceiling height is promised to anyone.

## Common mistakes

- **Promising a ceiling height before coordinating services.** The most common
  cause of a mid-project redesign.
- **Assessing the plenum at an easy location.** The worst crossing governs.
- **Specifying a raised floor without checking the plenum.** Both consume the
  same slab-to-slab dimension.
- **Leaving no access allowance.** Services that cannot be reached still need
  maintaining, and the ceiling gets damaged getting to them.
- **Ignoring the void at property selection.** Slab-to-slab height should be a
  question asked before a lease is signed, since nothing downstream can fix it.

## Standards referenced

Building services provisions in **NBC 2016, Part 8**, and fire and life safety
provisions covering concealed spaces in **NBC 2016, Part 4**. What is required
within a ceiling void for a specific building must be determined by the
project's services and fire consultants and the authority having jurisdiction.
