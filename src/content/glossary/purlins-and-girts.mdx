## Why it matters

Roof and wall cladding is only as secure as the steel it is fixed to. Purlins
and girts carry wind suction, maintenance loads and the weight of the cladding
into the frames, and in storms they are often where failures begin.

## How it works in practice

| Member | Location | Typical section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purlin | Roof, between frames | Cold-formed Z or C |
| Girt | Wall, between columns | Cold-formed Z or C |
| Eave strut | Roof-to-wall edge | Cold-formed C |
| Sag rod | Between purlins | Restrains them sideways |

## Common mistakes

- **Uniform spacing across the roof.** Edge and corner zones see higher uplift.
- **Omitting sag rods or bracing.** Thin sections twist without restraint.
- **Hanging loads without checking.** Services, cable trays and solar panels add
  load purlins were not designed for.

## Standards referenced

Cold-formed members under **IS 801**. Wind loads under **IS 875 (Part 3)**.
