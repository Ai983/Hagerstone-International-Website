  - question: "And how is it different from snagging?"
    answer: "Snagging is visual and about finish quality — a scratched door, a misaligned tile. Commissioning is about performance and is largely invisible. A space can be snag-free and badly commissioned, which is exactly the building that looks immaculate on handover day and generates complaints for a year."
  - question: "What does air balancing actually mean?"
    answer: "Adjusting dampers and settings so each space receives the airflow the design allocated to it, and the total matches the system's design. Without it, air follows the path of least resistance — rooms near the plant are over-supplied and distant rooms are starved, regardless of what the equipment schedule says. It is the single most common gap in an office handover."
  - question: "Why does commissioning get squeezed?"
    answer: "Because it sits at the end of the programme, after every preceding delay has accumulated, and because it is invisible to anyone walking the floor. When a date is fixed and the works are late, commissioning is the easiest thing to compress — and the consequences do not appear until the building is occupied, by which time the cause is arguable."
  - question: "What evidence should a client receive?"
    answer: "Recorded results, not assurances: airflow readings against design figures room by room, water flow rates, electrical test results, control and interface test records, fire system test certificates, and the commissioning method statement they were produced against. If the only evidence is a signed statement that commissioning was done, it effectively was not."
  - question: "What about system interfaces?"
    answer: "This is where commissioning most often falls short. ## Why it matters on a project

Most post-occupancy complaints about a new office are not construction defects.
They are commissioning gaps.

"Half the floor is cold and the other half is warm." "The meeting room is
stuffy." "The lights do not respond to the sensors." In almost every case the
equipment is correct, installed and working — it has simply never been set up to
deliver what the design intended.

That is why commissioning deserves attention from the client side, even though
it is the least visible part of a fit-out.

## Three different activities that get confused

| Activity | Question it answers | Visible? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Testing | Does this equipment work? | Partly |
| Commissioning | Does the system deliver the design intent? | No |
| Snagging | Does the finish look right? | Entirely |

A project can pass the first and third and fail the second completely, and
nothing on a walk-through would reveal it.

## The parts most often missed

- **Air balancing.** Rooms get the airflow the design allocated, measured and
  recorded. Without it, distribution is decided by pipe and duct resistance
  rather than by design.
- **Control strategy.** Not whether controls function, but whether they behave
  as intended — setpoints, schedules, sensor response, sequencing.
- **Interfaces between systems.** Fire alarm to air handling, dampers, access
  control, generator changeover. These belong to no single contractor and are
  skipped unless scheduled and witnessed.
- **Fresh air quantities.** Frequently assumed rather than measured, with
  ventilation rates commonly referenced to **ASHRAE 62.1**.
- **Electrical testing records**, in line with the installation practice adopted,
  commonly with reference to **IS 732**.

Testing and commissioning provisions for building services sit within
**NBC 2016, Part 8**, and the specific regime for a project must be set by the
MEP consultant.

## What to insist on

Ask for **numbers, not statements**. A commissioning record should show measured
values against design values, room by room and system by system, with the date
and who took them.

Ask for the **cause-and-effect test record** for interfaces, witnessed.

And put commissioning in the programme as a duration with a start date, not as
an activity that happens in whatever time remains.

## Common mistakes

- **Compressing it when the programme slips.** The consequences surface after
  occupation, when they are hardest to attribute.
- **Accepting a signature instead of readings.** No data means no commissioning.
- **Assuming interfaces were tested.** They belong to nobody by default.
- **Occupying before commissioning completes.** Balancing a floor full of people
  and furniture is far harder than balancing an empty one.
- **Treating snag-free as proof.** Appearance and performance are unrelated here.

## Standards referenced

Testing and commissioning provisions for building services in
**NBC 2016, Part 8**; ventilation rates commonly referenced to **ASHRAE 62.1**;
electrical installation practice in **IS 732**. The commissioning scope, method
and acceptance criteria for a specific project must be established by the
project's MEP consultant.
