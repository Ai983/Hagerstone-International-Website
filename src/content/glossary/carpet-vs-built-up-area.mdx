## Why it matters on a fit-out project

This is the most common source of budget confusion on Indian office projects,
and it usually surfaces at the worst moment — when a fit-out quote arrives and
appears far higher per square foot than expected.

Often nothing is wrong with the quote. The tenant is comparing a rate priced on
carpet area against a floor plate they think of in super built-up terms. On a
30% loading factor, the same total cost looks 30% more expensive per square foot
simply because of which denominator is used.

## How it is specified in practice

| Term | What it includes | Normally used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carpet area | Usable floor inside the walls | Fit-out pricing, furniture layout, seat counts |
| Built-up area | Carpet plus internal and external walls | Construction and structural discussion |
| Super built-up / chargeable | Built-up plus a share of common areas | Rent, maintenance charges, deposits |

**Loading factor** is the gap between carpet and chargeable area, expressed as a
percentage. A 10,000 sq ft chargeable floor at 30% loading gives roughly 7,700
sq ft of carpet — and that smaller number is what your fit-out, your workstation
count and your HVAC tonnage are all actually based on.

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing two fit-out quotes on different bases.** If one contractor prices
  on carpet and another on chargeable, the cheaper-looking rate can be the more
  expensive job. Always confirm the denominator before comparing.
- **Planning headcount from chargeable area.** Seat counts come from carpet
  area. Using the leased figure overstates capacity by the whole loading factor.
- **Assuming loading is standardised.** It is not. It varies building to
  building and is a negotiable lease term.
- **Sizing HVAC on the wrong number.** Cooling load follows the space you
  actually occupy and condition. Using chargeable area oversizes the plant and
  wastes both capital and running cost.
- **Not getting the definition written into the lease.** Terms vary between
  landlords. The lease should define exactly what is included, not rely on the
  common meaning.

## Standards referenced

**RERA 2016**, Section 2(k), gives the statutory definition of carpet area:
the net usable floor area within a unit, excluding external walls, service
shafts, balconies and verandas, but including internal partition walls.
