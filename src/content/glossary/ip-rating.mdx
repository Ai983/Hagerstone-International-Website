## Why it matters on a project

IP ratings appear on almost every electrical and lighting item in a
specification, and they are among the most casually copied numbers in the
industry — carried over from a previous project, or chosen because a higher
number feels safer.

The consequence of getting it wrong is not usually immediate. It is corrosion,
moisture ingress and equipment failure appearing a year or two later, in
locations that are inconvenient to reach.

## Reading the code

| Position | Protects against | Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First digit | Solid objects and dust | No protection → dust-tight |
| Second digit | Water | No protection → immersion |

The second digit is the one most often misread. The water tests describe
**different conditions** — dripping, spraying, splashing, jets, immersion — and
they are not a single scale where a higher number covers everything below it.
An enclosure tested for immersion is not automatically suitable for
high-pressure jet cleaning unless it is marked for both.

Classification is defined by **IEC 60529**, with **IS 12063** as the
corresponding Indian classification.

## Installation decides whether the rating is real

This is the part that matters most on site.

An IP-rated enclosure achieves its rating **as tested and correctly assembled**.
It loses it when:

- A hole is drilled without a sealed gland
- The wrong gland is used, or one is left off
- A gasket is omitted, damaged or misaligned
- The lid is over-tightened and distorted, or under-tightened
- The enclosure is installed in an orientation it was not tested in

None of those show up at handover. All of them show up later as moisture inside
an enclosure that was specified correctly.

## What IP does not cover

- **Impact.** A separate classification; a sealed enclosure can still be fragile.
- **Corrosion.** Material and finish matter separately, particularly in coastal
  or chemically aggressive environments.
- **Heat.** Sealing an enclosure restricts cooling, and equipment inside may need
  the heat considered.
- **UV.** Plastic enclosures outdoors degrade under sun regardless of IP rating.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming higher is always better.** The water tests are different tests.
- **Copying ratings from a previous project.** Exposure differs by location.
- **Breaking the rating during installation.** Glands and gaskets are part of it.
- **Specifying for the room, not the position.** A fitting under an open soffit
  faces different conditions from one in the middle of the same space.
- **Forgetting impact where equipment gets knocked.** IP says nothing about it.
- **Ignoring the cleaning regime.** A space that will be hosed down is a very
  different requirement from one that is mopped.

## Standards referenced

Degrees of protection provided by enclosures in **IEC 60529** and **IS 12063**;
electrical installation practice in **IS 732**. The required rating for a
specific location and installation must be determined by the project's
electrical consultant.
