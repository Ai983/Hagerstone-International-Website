## Why it matters on a facade project

After Grenfell, facade cladding fire performance stopped being a specification
detail and became the first question asked in a fire review. The panels look
identical from outside — the only thing separating a compliant facade from a
dangerous one is the material hidden between the two aluminium skins.

It is also one of the easiest places on a project to be substituted without
anyone noticing until a certificate is requested.

## How it is specified in practice

| Core grade | Mineral content | Typical use | Relative cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PE (polyethylene) | None | Signage, interior, low-rise non-critical | Baseline |
| FR (fire-retardant) | ~70% mineral filler | Common commercial specification | Higher |
| A2 (limited combustibility) | ~90%+ mineral | High-rise, hospitals, high-occupancy | Highest |

Beyond the panel itself, the fire performance of a facade also depends on what
sits behind it — cavity barriers, the insulation grade, and how the cavity is
compartmented at each floor. A compliant panel over an unstopped cavity is not a
compliant facade.

## Common mistakes

- **Specifying "FR ACP" and stopping there.** FR is a family, not a grade.
  Without naming the EN 13501-1 classification and requiring the test
  certificate for that exact product and thickness, the specification is not
  enforceable.
- **Ignoring the cavity.** A ventilated ACP facade has a continuous air gap
  behind it, which behaves like a chimney in a fire unless cavity barriers stop
  it at each compartment line.
- **Accepting a brochure instead of a certificate.** Brochures describe product
  families. Certificates describe tested assemblies. Only one of those is
  evidence.
- **Letting the core be substituted at supply stage.** The panel that arrives on
  site should be verifiable against the panel that was approved. Cut sections and
  batch traceability are the practical check.
- **Assuming thicker aluminium skin improves fire performance.** It does not.
  The skin is 0.5mm of aluminium either way; the core decides the outcome.

## Standards referenced

Fire and life safety for the building follows **NBC 2016, Part 4**. Panel
reaction-to-fire classification is expressed under **EN 13501-1**, which is the
system the A2 / B1 grades come from. Metal cladding material requirements sit
under **IS 6248**.
