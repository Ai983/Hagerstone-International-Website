## Why it matters

The frame is most of a PEB's steel tonnage, and tonnage drives cost. Tapering is
what makes PEB economical: by varying the web depth along each member, the
designer removes steel that a uniform section would carry for no structural
reason.

## How it works in practice

| Feature | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Built-up section | Flanges and web cut from plate and welded |
| Variable depth | Deeper at the knee and ridge, shallower at mid-rafter |
| Bolted connections | Frames bolted together on site |
| Design basis | Member forces from the building's actual loads |

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing PEB and conventional steel on rate per tonne.** PEB often uses less
  steel, so tonnage comparisons mislead.
- **Adding crane or roof loads after design.** Tapered members are optimised for
  the original loads and cannot absorb large additions.
- **Ignoring connection quality.** Bolted knees and splices must be tightened and
  inspected properly.
