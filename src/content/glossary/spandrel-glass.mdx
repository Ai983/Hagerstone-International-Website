## Why it matters on a facade project

A glazed elevation is not glass all the way down. Behind every apparently
continuous run of glazing sits a floor slab, a ceiling plenum and the services
threaded through it — typically 900mm to 1,400mm of structure and duct that
nobody wants to look at. **Spandrel glass is what hides it.**

Get it wrong and it shows in two expensive ways: panels that crack in the first
summer, and an elevation where the spandrel bands read as a visibly different
colour from the vision glass under certain light.

## How it is specified in practice

| Parameter | Typical position | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glass treatment | Heat-strengthened, occasionally toughened | The cavity behind traps heat; annealed glass fails on thermal stress |
| Opacifier | Ceramic frit or opaque film | Frit is more durable; film is cheaper and easier to match |
| Cavity ventilation | Vented or sealed, decided early | Drives both condensation risk and panel temperature |
| Back-up insulation | Mineral wool, thickness per ECBC | Counted as opaque assembly, not glazing |

## Common mistakes

- **Specifying annealed glass to save cost.** The cavity behind a south-facing
  spandrel can run 30-40°C hotter than the vision glass. Annealed panels crack,
  and replacing them means returning to a sealed facade at height.
- **Leaving the colour match untested.** Vision and spandrel glass reflect
  differently because one has a lit interior behind it and the other has a dark
  cavity. Match them on a mock-up, in daylight, not from a sample board.
- **Forgetting insulation behind the spandrel.** It is opaque assembly under
  ECBC, and it is usually the cheapest place to recover an envelope shortfall.
- **Deciding vented versus sealed late.** It changes the panel temperature, the
  glass grade and the condensation strategy — all of which are fixed by the time
  the facade is ordered.

## Standards referenced

Safety glass requirements follow **IS 2553 (Part 1)**. Where spandrel forms part
of the opaque envelope, its assembly U-value is assessed under **ECBC 2017**.
