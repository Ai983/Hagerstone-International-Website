## Why it matters on a project

Earthing and bonding are the parts of an electrical installation that do nothing
at all until the day they are needed, and then determine whether a fault is an
inconvenience or an injury.

They are also the parts most easily damaged by subsequent work, because they
consist largely of conductors connected to metalwork that later trades cut,
move and replace.

## The two functions

| | Earthing | Bonding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Let fault current return so protection operates | Keep touchable metalwork at similar potential |
| Protects against | Prolonged fault, fire, equipment damage | Dangerous touch voltage during a fault |
| Connects to | Earth electrode system | Other metalwork and the main earth terminal |
| Failure mode | Protection does not operate fast enough | A person bridges two different potentials |
| Substitutes for the other? | No | No |

## What fit-out work tends to break

This is where the subject becomes practical for anyone running an interiors
project:

- A bonding conductor disconnected to move a pipe, not reconnected
- A metallic pipe section replaced with plastic, breaking continuity that
  something downstream relied on
- New metalwork — partitions, raised floors, trunking — installed without being
  brought into the bonding arrangement where required
- Earth conductors damaged or omitted at new distribution boards
- Earth pits built over, paved across or made inaccessible for testing

None of these produces a symptom. The installation energises and works. The
deficiency is only discovered by inspection and test, or by a fault.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating earthing and bonding as the same thing.** They protect against
  different events.
- **Disturbing bonding during fit-out without recording it.** What is not
  recorded is not reinstated.
- **Building over earth pits.** They need to remain accessible for testing and
  maintenance.
- **Testing once at handover and never again.** Earth resistance changes with
  ground conditions over time.
- **Improvising separate earths for IT equipment.** Without proper design this
  can create the hazard it was meant to avoid.
- **No test records at handover.** Inspection and test documentation is how
  anyone later knows what was actually installed.

## Standards referenced

Earthing practice in **IS 3043**; electrical installation practice in
**IS 732**; building services provisions in **NBC 2016, Part 8**. Earthing
system design, bonding requirements, test values and the testing regime for a
specific installation must be established by the project's electrical consultant
and verified by inspection and test.
