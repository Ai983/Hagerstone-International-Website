  - question: "What is the difference between an FCU and an AHU?"
    answer: "Scale and role. An air handling unit is a larger central unit serving many spaces, usually handling outdoor air, filtration and conditioning together, and distributing through a duct network. A fan coil unit is a small terminal device serving one room or a small zone, recirculating that space's air across a coil. A building commonly uses both — an AHU or treated fresh air unit for outdoor air, FCUs for local cooling and control."
  - question: "Does an FCU bring in fresh air?"
    answer: "Not on its own. It recirculates room air across a coil. Outdoor air must be introduced separately, usually by a treated fresh air unit ducted either into the space or into the FCU's return. Forgetting this is one of the most common causes of a building that cools well and still feels stuffy, and it is a design omission rather than an equipment fault."
  - question: "Why is condensate drainage such a frequent problem?"
    answer: "Because the coil is below dew point, water forms continuously, and the drain has to carry it away by gravity from a unit hidden above a ceiling. Inadequate fall, a blocked or unprimed trap, or a drain run that dips will back the tray up and the water appears as a ceiling stain in the room below. It is the single most common FCU service complaint and almost always an installation issue, not an equipment one."
  - question: "What access does an FCU need?"
    answer: "Enough to change filters routinely and to reach the coil, drain pan, trap, valves and motor for service. That means a properly sized access panel positioned under the unit, not a token hatch somewhere nearby. Units installed without practical access get neglected, and a neglected filter degrades both airflow and the coil behind it."
  - question: "Are FCUs noisy?"
    answer: "They can be, because the fan is in the ceiling directly above occupants. Noise depends on unit selection, fan speed, whether the unit is oversized and running hard, duct and grille design, and vibration isolation. Selecting on capacity alone without checking sound data is how a meeting room ends up with an audible hum nobody can remove afterwards."

## Why it matters on a project

Fan coil units are the part of an HVAC system occupants interact with most and
notice only when something goes wrong — a ceiling stain, a hum in a meeting
room, a room that never quite cools.

Almost all of those outcomes are decided during installation and coordination,
not during equipment selection.

## What is inside one

| Component | Function | Where it causes trouble |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fan | Moves room air across the coil | Noise, especially if oversized |
| Coil | Chilled or hot water heat exchange | Fouls if the filter is neglected |
| Filter | Protects the coil, cleans recirculated air | Needs routine, easy access |
| Drain pan and trap | Collects and removes condensate | Leaks, ceiling staining |
| Control valve | Modulates water flow to the coil | Control quality, energy use |

## The three details that decide satisfaction

**Condensate drainage.** Correct continuous fall, a properly formed and primed
trap, and no dips. This is where most FCU complaints originate.

**Access.** A correctly sized access panel directly below the unit. If filters
are awkward to change, they will not be changed.

**Noise.** Check sound data against the room's requirement, particularly for
meeting rooms and cabins. An oversized unit running at high speed to meet a
control setpoint is noisier than a correctly selected one.

## Fresh air is separate

An FCU conditions air; it does not ventilate. Outdoor air for occupants comes
from a separate treated fresh air supply, sized against the ventilation rates
adopted for the project — commonly referenced to **ASHRAE 62.1**, within the
framework of **NBC 2016, Part 8**.

Equipment efficiency and control requirements for commercial buildings are
addressed in **ECBC 2017**. Selection and sizing are the MEP consultant's
output.

## Common mistakes

- **Omitting the fresh air system.** The building cools and still feels stale.
- **Condensate runs with inadequate fall.** Produces ceiling stains, usually
  months after handover.
- **Token access panels.** Maintenance that is difficult does not happen.
- **Selecting on capacity without sound data.** Meeting rooms suffer most.
- **Oversizing.** Short cycling, poor humidity control and more noise.
- **Moving units during ceiling coordination without rechecking drains.** The
  fall that worked on the original layout may not survive the new position.

## Standards referenced

Building services provisions in **NBC 2016, Part 8**; ventilation rates commonly
referenced to **ASHRAE 62.1**; HVAC equipment and control requirements in
**ECBC 2017**. Equipment selection, sizing and system design must be established
by the project's MEP consultant.
