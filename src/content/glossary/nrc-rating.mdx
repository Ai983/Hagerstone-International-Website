## Why it matters

Open-plan offices fail most often on noise, and the fix is largely absorption:
ceilings, carpet and wall panels that stop voices reflecting across the floor.
NRC is the number that tells you how much a material will help.

## How it works in practice

| NRC | Behaviour | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.0 – 0.2 | Reflective | Plasterboard, glass, stone |
| 0.4 – 0.6 | Moderate absorption | Standard ceiling tiles |
| 0.7 – 0.9 | High absorption | Acoustic tiles, baffles, fabric panels |

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing absorption with insulation.** High NRC does not stop sound passing
  through a wall.
- **Hard finishes everywhere.** Glass, stone and plasterboard together make a
  loud office.
- **Ignoring the ceiling.** It is usually the most effective place to absorb.

## Standards referenced

Sound absorption is measured in a reverberation room under **ISO 354**.
