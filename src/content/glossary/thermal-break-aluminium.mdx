## Why it matters on a project

Aluminium is roughly a thousand times more conductive than the polyamide strip
that breaks it. That single fact is why an unbroken aluminium frame behaves like
a heat pipe through the wall — and why, in a chilled office in a humid Indian
summer, water appears on the inside face of the frame while the glass stays
clear.

Occupants read that as a leak. It is not; it is condensation on a cold metal
surface, and it is a specification decision made months earlier.

## How it is specified in practice

| Parameter | Typical position | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Break material | Polyamide 6.6, glass-fibre reinforced | Low conductivity, and strong enough to be structural |
| Break depth | Commonly 14-34mm | Deeper break, better performance and higher cost |
| Assembly | Rolled-in and knurled, factory | The composite must act as one section structurally |
| Alloy | 6063 T6 | Standard architectural extrusion alloy |
| Gasket | EPDM | Durable under UV and thermal cycling |
| Verification | System U-value from the system supplier | Frame performance is a tested property, not a claim |

## Common mistakes

- **Specifying thermally broken glass and a plain frame.** Paying for
  high-performance DGU and then bridging the assembly with bare aluminium wastes
  most of the benefit at the frame.
- **Assuming it is only for cold climates.** In India the driver is usually
  cooling load and condensation control, not heating.
- **Ignoring the break depth.** "Thermally broken" covers a wide performance
  range. A 14mm break and a 34mm break are not the same product.
- **Breaking the frame and forgetting the fixing.** Steel brackets bridging
  around the break reintroduce the path the break was meant to interrupt.
- **Mixing systems.** Profile, gasket, hardware and drainage are designed as one
  assembly. Substituting one part of it invalidates the tested performance.
